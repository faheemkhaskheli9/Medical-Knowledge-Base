## Public health priorities
| Topic | Key points |
|-------|------------|
| **XDR typhoid** | Since 2016, mainly Sindh: resistant to chloramphenicol, ampicillin, co-trimoxazole, fluoroquinolones, ceftriaxone. Azithromycin (uncomplicated) or carbapenem (complicated) per culture. Typhoid conjugate vaccine in EPI. |
| **Dengue** | Seasonal (post-monsoon). Paracetamol for fever; **avoid aspirin and NSAIDs** (bleeding risk). Warning signs: abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding, lethargy, falling platelets with rising hematocrit. |
| **Polio** | One of two countries where wild poliovirus remains endemic (with Afghanistan) — complete OPV/IPV and campaign doses. |
| **Hepatitis B and C** | High prevalence; screen, especially with history of injections/transfusions; HCV is curable with direct-acting antivirals. Unsafe injections and reuse of syringes are major risk factors. |
| **Tuberculosis** | High burden; cough ≥ 2 weeks → test; complete full treatment course (DOTS). |
| **Thalassemia** | Common carrier state; premarital/antenatal screening recommended. |
| **Diarrhea in children** | ORS + zinc (WHO); continue feeding; antibiotics only for bloody diarrhea (dysentery) or cholera. |
| **Heat stroke** | Summer heatwaves — see [first aid](../first-aid/first-aid.md). |
| **Snake bite, rabies** | Antivenom and rabies post-exposure prophylaxis via designated hospitals; wash dog bites with soap and water for 15 minutes. |

## Medication safety concerns
- Antibiotics and other prescription drugs are widely available without prescription → advise against self-medication.
- Substandard and falsified medicines — buy from licensed pharmacies; report to DRAP.
- Injection overuse — oral therapy is preferred where effective.
