# Drug Allergy and Cross-Reactivity

## Penicillin allergy
- About 10% of people report a penicillin allergy; fewer than 1% are truly allergic when tested. Unverified labels lead to broader, less effective antibiotics — refer for **allergy evaluation / delabeling**.
- Record the reaction type: immediate (urticaria, angioedema, anaphylaxis) vs delayed benign rash vs severe delayed (SJS/TEN, DRESS).
- **Severe delayed reactions (SJS/TEN, DRESS):** avoid the drug class; specialist advice before any beta-lactam.

## Beta-lactam cross-reactivity
| Alternative | Cross-reactivity with penicillin | Notes |
|-------------|---------------------------------|-------|
| Cephalosporins | Low (~1–2%) overall | Driven by similar **R1 side chains**, not the beta-lactam ring |
| Cephalexin, cefadroxil, cefaclor | Higher | Share side chain with amoxicillin/ampicillin |
| Cefazolin | Very low | Unique side chain |
| Carbapenems | < 1% | Generally usable with monitoring |
| Aztreonam | Negligible | Except **ceftazidime** allergy (shared side chain) |

## Other common questions
| Belief | Fact |
|--------|------|
| Sulfa antibiotic allergy means avoiding furosemide, thiazides, celecoxib | Cross-reactivity with non-antibiotic sulfonamides is not supported; usually safe |
| Shellfish/iodine allergy means avoiding contrast | No relationship; iodine is not an allergen |
| Egg allergy prevents influenza vaccination | Any age-appropriate influenza vaccine can be given (CDC); no extra precautions beyond standard |
| Aspirin sensitivity | NSAID-exacerbated respiratory disease cross-reacts with other COX-1 inhibitors; selective COX-2 inhibitors usually tolerated |
| Codeine "allergy" (nausea, itching) | Often an intolerance or pseudoallergy, not IgE allergy — document accurately |
