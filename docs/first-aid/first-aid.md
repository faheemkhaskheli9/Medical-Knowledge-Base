# First Aid

!!! danger "Always call emergency services first"
    Ensure scene safety, call for help, and follow the dispatcher's instructions. Formal training is strongly recommended.

## CPR — unresponsive and not breathing normally
| | Adult | Child | Infant (< 1 year) |
|---|-------|-------|-------------------|
| Compression rate | 100–120/min | 100–120/min | 100–120/min |
| Depth | 5–6 cm | ~5 cm (⅓ chest depth) | ~4 cm (⅓ chest depth) |
| Ratio (trained rescuer) | 30:2 | 30:2 (one rescuer) | 30:2 (one rescuer) |

- Untrained or unwilling to give breaths: **hands-only CPR** (continuous compressions) for adults.
- Use an **AED** as soon as available and follow its prompts.
- Gasping (agonal breathing) is **not** normal breathing — start CPR.

## Choking (conscious)
- Encourage coughing while it is effective.
- **Adult/child:** if cough ineffective, 5 back blows then 5 abdominal thrusts; repeat (some guidelines use abdominal thrusts alone).
- **Infant:** 5 back blows then 5 chest thrusts; **no abdominal thrusts**.
- If unresponsive: start CPR; check mouth for visible object before breaths.

## Severe bleeding
Firm direct pressure; add dressings without removing soaked ones; tourniquet for life-threatening limb bleeding when trained/available; note the time applied.

## Burns
Cool with cool running water for **20 minutes** (useful up to 3 hours after); remove jewelry and clothing not stuck to the burn; cover loosely with cling film or clean non-fluffy dressing. **No** ice, butter, toothpaste, or oils. Seek care for burns that are large, deep, on face/hands/genitals/joints, electrical, chemical, or in young children/older adults.

## Anaphylaxis
Use adrenaline (epinephrine) auto-injector into the outer thigh; call emergency services; lie flat with legs raised (sit up if breathing is difficult; pregnant — left side); second dose after 5 minutes if no improvement.

## Seizure
Time it; protect the head; move hazards away; **nothing in the mouth**; recovery position after jerking stops. Call emergency services if > 5 minutes, repeated seizures, first seizure, injury, pregnancy, or breathing problems.

## Low blood sugar (conscious, able to swallow)
15–20 g fast sugar (glucose tablets, juice); recheck after 15 minutes; then a snack. Unconscious → nothing by mouth, emergency services.

## Heat stroke
Hot, confused, or collapsed → call emergency services; **rapid cooling** (cold-water immersion if possible, otherwise wet and fan, ice packs to neck/armpits/groin).

## Snake bite
Keep the person still and calm; immobilize the limb; remove rings/tight items; go to hospital for antivenom. **No** cutting, sucking, ice, or arterial tourniquet. Pressure immobilization bandaging applies only to specific species (e.g. Australian elapids) — follow local guidance.
