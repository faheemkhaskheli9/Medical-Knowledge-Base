# Type 2 Diabetes (adults)

**ICD-10:** E11

## Diagnosis (ADA)
Any of: HbA1c ≥ 6.5% (48 mmol/mol), fasting glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L), 2-h OGTT ≥ 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L), or random glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL with classic symptoms. Without symptoms, confirm with a repeat test. See [lab ranges](../clinical-reference/lab-reference-ranges.md).

## Red flags
- Vomiting, abdominal pain, rapid breathing, ketones → DKA (can occur with SGLT2 inhibitors even at normal glucose — "euglycemic DKA").
- Very high glucose with dehydration and confusion → hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state.
- Hypoglycemia with confusion, seizure, or inability to self-treat.

## Management
- **Lifestyle:** nutrition therapy, weight management, ≥ 150 min/week activity, smoking cessation.
- **Metformin** is generally first-line (see [metformin](../medicines/monographs/metformin.md)).
- **Independent of HbA1c or metformin use**, add agents with proven organ benefit:
  - Established ASCVD or high CV risk → GLP-1 receptor agonist or SGLT2 inhibitor with proven CV benefit.
  - Heart failure → SGLT2 inhibitor.
  - CKD (eGFR ≥ 20 and/or albuminuria) → SGLT2 inhibitor; GLP-1 RA as additional option.
- Obesity → GLP-1 RA or dual GIP/GLP-1 RA (tirzepatide) supports weight loss.
- Insulin when HbA1c very high, symptomatic hyperglycemia, or catabolic features.

## Targets (individualize)
- HbA1c < 7% (53 mmol/mol) for many non-pregnant adults; less strict (e.g. < 8%) for older/frail adults or hypoglycemia risk.
- BP < 130/80 mmHg; statin for most adults aged 40–75.

## Hypoglycemia (ADA)
| Level | Glucose |
|-------|---------|
| 1 | < 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L) |
| 2 | < 54 mg/dL (3.0 mmol/L) |
| 3 | Severe — needs assistance from another person |

Treat conscious patients with 15–20 g fast-acting glucose; recheck after 15 minutes; repeat if still low. Unconscious → glucagon, emergency services.

## Sick-day guidance
During acute illness with dehydration, temporarily hold medicines that raise risk: SGLT2 inhibitors, metformin, ACE inhibitors/ARBs, diuretics, NSAIDs (UK mnemonic "SADMANS" also includes sulfonylureas). Do not stop insulin; monitor glucose and ketones more often.

## Annual monitoring
HbA1c (every 3–6 months), eye screening, foot examination, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, eGFR, lipids, BP, vitamin B12 if on metformin.
