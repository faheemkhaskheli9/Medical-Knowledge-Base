# Mirtazapine

**Class / ATC:** Noradrenergic and specific serotonergic antidepressant (NaSSA); alpha-2 antagonist, also blocks 5-HT2, 5-HT3 and H1 receptors — N06AX11. Sedating; increases appetite and weight.

## Dosing (oral, adult)
| Population | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Adults | 15 mg once daily at bedtime; increase if needed up to 45 mg once daily. Change doses at intervals of at least 1–2 weeks (FDA). UK: 15–30 mg at bedtime, max 45 mg daily (BNF) |
| Older adults | Start 15 mg; titrate cautiously (reduced clearance) |
| Renal or hepatic impairment | Clearance reduced — use with caution, titrate carefully (FDA, BNF) |
| Children and adolescents | Not recommended for depression |

Orodispersible tablets dissolve on the tongue; the dose is the same.

## Contraindications
- Use of an **MAOI** within the past 14 days, or starting an MAOI within 14 days of stopping mirtazapine; linezolid or IV methylene blue (FDA).
- Hypersensitivity to mirtazapine.

## Warnings
- **Boxed warning — suicidality** in children, adolescents and young adults (under 25) on antidepressants; monitor closely early on ([crisis support](../../mental-health/crisis-support.md)).
- **Agranulocytosis / neutropenia** (rare): stop and check a full blood count if fever, sore throat, mouth ulcers or other signs of infection.
- **QT prolongation and torsades de pointes** reported, mainly in overdose or with other QT-prolonging drugs.
- Serotonin syndrome with other serotonergic drugs.
- Hyponatremia (SIADH), especially older adults.
- Severe cutaneous reactions including DRESS and SJS/TEN.
- May trigger mania; screen for bipolar disorder.
- Discontinuation symptoms — taper rather than stop abruptly.
- Weight gain, raised cholesterol; sedation (caution with driving).

## Interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| MAOIs, linezolid | Contraindicated — serotonin syndrome |
| SSRIs ([sertraline](sertraline.md)), tramadol, triptans, lithium | Serotonin syndrome risk |
| Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (ketoconazole, clarithromycin) | Raise mirtazapine levels |
| CYP3A4 inducers (carbamazepine, phenytoin, rifampicin) | Lower mirtazapine levels |
| QT-prolonging drugs | Additive QT risk |
| Alcohol, [benzodiazepines](diazepam.md), opioids | Additive sedation |

## Adverse effects
- Common: drowsiness, increased appetite, weight gain, dry mouth, dizziness, constipation.
- Serious: agranulocytosis, hyponatremia, QT prolongation, serotonin syndrome, suicidality.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Specialist decision on risk versus benefit; observe the newborn if used late in pregnancy. Low levels in breast milk; considered acceptable — monitor the infant for drowsiness (LactMed).

## Monitoring
Mood and suicidality in early weeks; weight; full blood count if signs of infection; sodium in older adults or with other risk factors.

## Overdose
Usually drowsiness, confusion and tachycardia; QT prolongation possible, especially with co-ingestants. Supportive care, ECG. See [antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md).

## Patient counselling
Take at bedtime. Benefit usually takes 2–4 weeks. Expect drowsiness and increased appetite. Do not stop suddenly. Avoid alcohol; do not drive if drowsy. Report fever, sore throat or mouth ulcers promptly. Seek help urgently for thoughts of self-harm.
