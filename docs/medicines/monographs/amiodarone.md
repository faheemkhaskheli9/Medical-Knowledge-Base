# Amiodarone

**Class / ATC:** Class III antiarrhythmic (also class I, II and IV actions) — C01BD01 — **high-alert medication** ([high-alert list](../high-alert-medications.md)). Iodine-containing. Very long half-life (amiodarone about 15–142 days; active metabolite desethylamiodarone about 14–75 days), so **adverse effects and interactions persist for weeks to months after stopping**.

## Indications
US oral label: life-threatening recurrent ventricular arrhythmias (ventricular fibrillation, hemodynamically unstable ventricular tachycardia) when other drugs are ineffective or not tolerated. Widely used (off-label in the US) for atrial fibrillation rhythm control. IV use in cardiac arrest and acute arrhythmias follows resuscitation guidelines.

## Dosing (oral, adult, FDA tablets)
| Phase | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Loading | 800–1600 mg/day until initial response (usually 1–3 weeks) |
| Step-down | 600–800 mg/day for 1 month |
| Maintenance | Usually 400 mg/day; use the lowest effective dose |

- Start in hospital with continuous ECG monitoring and resuscitation available (boxed warning). UK BNF regimens differ — check the BNF.
- IV dosing: follow the label and resuscitation or local protocols; infuse through a central line where possible for longer infusions.
- No renal adjustment. Hepatic impairment: use with caution and monitor.

## Contraindications
- Cardiogenic shock.
- Sick sinus syndrome, second- or third-degree AV block, or bradycardia causing syncope, **unless a functioning pacemaker** is present.
- Hypersensitivity to amiodarone or its components, **including iodine**.
- Some non-US labels also contraindicate thyroid dysfunction, hepatitis or interstitial lung abnormalities — check local labeling.

## Warnings / boxed warnings
- **Pulmonary toxicity** (boxed): hypersensitivity pneumonitis or interstitial/alveolar pneumonitis, reported in up to 17% in some series and fatal in about 10% of those cases. Baseline chest X-ray and pulmonary function tests; investigate new cough or breathlessness.
- **Hepatic injury** (boxed): monitor liver enzymes; reduce dose or stop if above 3 times normal (or doubling of an elevated baseline).
- **Worsened arrhythmia** (boxed), including torsades de pointes; QT prolongation; bradycardia and heart block.
- **Thyroid:** hypothyroidism or hyperthyroidism (thyrotoxicosis can be severe). Thyroid function before treatment and periodically.
- **Eyes:** corneal microdeposits (common, reversible); optic neuropathy (rare, may cause blindness) — prompt eye review for visual changes.
- **Skin:** photosensitivity; blue-gray skin discoloration. Peripheral neuropathy.

## Major interactions
Inhibits P-gp and CYP1A2, CYP2C9, CYP2D6 and CYP3A; effects persist after stopping.

| Drug | Effect / action (FDA label) |
|------|--------|
| [Warfarin](warfarin.md) | INR roughly doubles within 3–4 days — reduce warfarin dose by one-third to one-half and monitor INR closely |
| [Digoxin](digoxin.md) | Raised digoxin levels — reduce digoxin dose by half or stop; monitor for toxicity |
| Simvastatin | Myopathy — limit simvastatin to 20 mg daily; consider another statin ([atorvastatin](atorvastatin.md) at lowest effective dose with monitoring) |
| **Sofosbuvir**-containing hepatitis C regimens | Symptomatic, sometimes fatal bradycardia — avoid; if unavoidable, cardiac monitoring per label |
| Beta-blockers ([bisoprolol](bisoprolol.md)), verapamil, diltiazem | Bradycardia, AV block, hypotension |
| Other QT-prolonging drugs (e.g. macrolides, fluoroquinolones, antipsychotics, [ondansetron](ondansetron.md)) | Torsades de pointes |
| [Phenytoin](phenytoin.md), ciclosporin, tacrolimus, [apixaban](apixaban.md)/[rivaroxaban](rivaroxaban.md) | Altered levels — monitor |

See [major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md).

## Adverse effects
- Common: nausea, constipation, fatigue, tremor, photosensitivity, corneal microdeposits, raised liver enzymes, thyroid function changes.
- Serious: pulmonary toxicity, hepatotoxicity, thyroid disease, proarrhythmia, bradycardia, optic neuropathy, peripheral neuropathy.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Can cause fetal harm: neonatal hypo- or hyperthyroidism, bradycardia, neurodevelopmental problems, preterm birth and growth restriction; amiodarone crosses the placenta. Use only when no alternative, with specialist advice. **Breastfeeding is not recommended** during treatment (label). See [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
Baseline: ECG, chest X-ray, pulmonary function, liver function, thyroid function, electrolytes (correct low potassium and magnesium). Ongoing: liver and thyroid function periodically (commonly every 6 months), ECG, eye review for visual symptoms, new respiratory symptoms, INR or digoxin level if co-prescribed.

## Overdose / antidote
Bradycardia, hypotension, AV block, QT prolongation and arrhythmias; possible liver injury. Supportive care with ECG monitoring; not removed by dialysis. Contact a poison center ([antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md)).

## Patient counselling points
Use high-factor sunscreen and cover up — skin becomes very sensitive to the sun. Report new cough or breathlessness, yellowing of skin or eyes, blurred or reduced vision, palpitations or fainting, and symptoms of an over- or under-active thyroid. Tell every prescriber and pharmacist you take amiodarone; interactions can last months after stopping. Keep blood test appointments.
