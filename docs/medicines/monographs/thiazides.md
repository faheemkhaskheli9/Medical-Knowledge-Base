# Thiazide and thiazide-like diuretics

**Class / ATC:** Thiazide — hydrochlorothiazide C03AA03, bendroflumethiazide C03AA01. Thiazide-like — indapamide C03BA11, chlortalidone (chlorthalidone) C03BA04.

**Mechanism:** Inhibit the Na⁺/Cl⁻ cotransporter in the distal convoluted tubule.

## Dosing (adult, oral, once daily in the morning)
| Drug | Hypertension dose |
|------|-------------------|
| Indapamide | 2.5 mg, or 1.5 mg modified-release (NICE preferred thiazide-like option) |
| Chlortalidone | 12.5–25 mg |
| Hydrochlorothiazide | 12.5–25 mg (max 50 mg; higher doses add adverse effects more than BP lowering) |
| Bendroflumethiazide | 2.5 mg (NICE: continue if already stable on it, otherwise prefer thiazide-like) |

NICE NG136 and ACC/AHA prefer **thiazide-like** diuretics (indapamide, chlortalidone) for new hypertension treatment. Often combined with an ACE inhibitor/ARB or calcium channel blocker. See [hypertension](../../conditions/hypertension.md).

Efficacy falls in advanced CKD; chlortalidone lowered BP in stage 4 CKD in the CLICK trial (NEJM 2021) — specialist use. Loop diuretics are used for edema ([furosemide](furosemide.md)).

## Contraindications
Refractory hypokalemia, hyponatremia or hypercalcemia; symptomatic hyperuricemia/gout (relative); Addison's disease; anuria; severe hepatic impairment (risk of hepatic encephalopathy). Labels list hypersensitivity to sulfonamide-derived drugs, although cross-reactivity with sulfonamide antibiotics is not supported (see [allergy cross-reactivity](../../allergy/cross-reactivity.md)).

## Warnings
- **Hyponatremia** — especially older women, low body weight, with SSRIs; can be severe.
- **Hypokalemia**, hypomagnesemia, hypercalcemia.
- Hyperuricemia and **gout** flares; raised glucose and lipids (small).
- Photosensitivity. **Hydrochlorothiazide: increased risk of non-melanoma skin cancer** with cumulative use (EMA 2018, FDA 2020) — sun protection, skin checks.
- Acute angle-closure glaucoma/myopia (rare, sulfonamide-type reaction) — stop.

## Interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Lithium | Raised lithium levels — avoid or monitor levels closely ([lithium](lithium.md)) |
| Digoxin | Hypokalemia increases digoxin toxicity ([digoxin](digoxin.md)) |
| NSAIDs | Reduced BP effect; AKI with ACE inhibitor/ARB ("triple whammy") |
| Other QT-prolonging drugs | Hypokalemia increases torsades risk |
| SSRIs, carbamazepine | Additive hyponatremia |

## Adverse effects
- Common: urinary frequency, dizziness, hypokalemia, raised uric acid, erectile dysfunction.
- Serious: severe hyponatremia (confusion, seizures), severe hypokalemia (arrhythmia), gout.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Not used for gestational hypertension or pre-eclampsia (may reduce plasma volume); continuing for pre-existing hypertension is a specialist decision and practice varies by region. High doses may suppress lactation.

## Monitoring
Sodium, potassium, creatinine/eGFR before starting, within a few weeks of starting or a dose change, and then at least annually; uric acid and glucose as indicated.

## Patient counselling
Take in the morning to avoid night-time urination. Hold during vomiting, diarrhea or dehydration (sick-day rule). Report muscle cramps, weakness, confusion, palpitations, or a painful swollen joint. Use sun protection.
