# Hydroxychloroquine

**Class / ATC:** Antimalarial (4-aminoquinoline), conventional synthetic DMARD — P01BA02. Doses below are hydroxychloroquine **sulfate** (200 mg sulfate = 155 mg base).

## Dosing (adult, oral)
| Indication | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Rheumatoid arthritis | 200–400 mg daily, as a single dose or 2 divided doses (FDA) |
| Systemic / discoid lupus erythematosus | 200–400 mg daily (FDA) |
| **Long-term maximum (retinal safety)** | **≤ 5 mg/kg actual body weight per day** (AAO 2016, RCOphth 2020) |
| Malaria prophylaxis (chloroquine-sensitive areas only) | 400 mg once weekly, starting 2 weeks before travel and continuing 4 weeks after leaving (FDA) |

Renal or hepatic impairment: use with caution; reduced eGFR raises the retinopathy risk. Take with food or milk.

## Contraindications
Hypersensitivity to 4-aminoquinolines. Pre-existing maculopathy (BNF: caution/avoid).

## Warnings
- **Retinopathy** (irreversible): risk rises with dose above 5 mg/kg/day, duration over 5 years, reduced eGFR and tamoxifen use. Baseline eye exam (within the first year), then annual screening after 5 years — or after 1 year if risk factors are present (AAO 2016, RCOphth 2020).
- **Cardiomyopathy** and **QT prolongation**/ventricular arrhythmias.
- **Hypoglycemia**, including in patients without diabetes.
- Neuropsychiatric effects, including suicidal behaviour; myopathy and neuropathy.
- Severe skin reactions (SJS/TEN, DRESS); may worsen psoriasis and porphyria.
- Hemolysis possible with G6PD deficiency.

## Interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Other QT-prolonging drugs (macrolides, fluoroquinolones, antipsychotics, citalopram, ondansetron) | Torsades risk ([major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md)) |
| Insulin, sulfonylureas | Hypoglycemia — may need lower antidiabetic doses |
| [Digoxin](digoxin.md) | Raised digoxin levels |
| Ciclosporin | Raised ciclosporin levels |
| Mefloquine and other drugs that lower seizure threshold | Seizure risk |
| Antacids, kaolin | Reduced absorption — separate doses by at least 4 hours |

## Adverse effects
- Common: nausea, diarrhea, abdominal pain, headache, rash, skin pigmentation.
- Serious: retinopathy, cardiomyopathy, arrhythmia, hypoglycemia, severe skin reactions, bone marrow suppression (rare).

## Pregnancy / lactation
Continuing hydroxychloroquine is recommended in pregnancy for patients with lupus (ACR 2020). Small amounts pass into breast milk; considered compatible with breastfeeding.

## Overdose
Dangerous, especially in children (small numbers of tablets can be fatal): rapid onset of cardiotoxicity, hypokalemia, seizures and collapse. Emergency treatment and poison center advice. Keep out of reach of children. See [antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md).

## Patient counselling
Benefits for arthritis build up over weeks to months. Attend eye screening; report blurred vision, difficulty reading or missing areas of vision. Report palpitations, fainting, low mood, muscle weakness or a rash. Know the signs of low blood sugar. Not recommended for COVID-19 (FDA revoked its emergency use authorisation in 2020).
