# Valproate (sodium valproate, valproic acid)

**Class / ATC:** Antiepileptic, mood stabilizer — N03AG01

!!! danger "Teratogen"
    Up to ~10% risk of major birth defects and ~30–40% risk of neurodevelopmental disorders with exposure in pregnancy. In the UK (MHRA 2024), valproate must **not be started** in patients under 55 years unless two specialists independently document that no other treatment is effective or tolerated. Women and girls of childbearing potential must be on a **Pregnancy Prevention Programme**.

## Dosing (epilepsy, adult — specialist-initiated)
Start ~10–15 mg/kg/day in divided doses; increase gradually; usual maintenance 20–30 mg/kg/day. Typical trough level 50–100 mg/L (correlates poorly with effect).

## Boxed warnings / serious risks
- **Hepatotoxicity** — highest in children < 2 years, polytherapy, and mitochondrial disease (POLG mutations — contraindicated).
- **Pancreatitis**.
- Teratogenicity (above). Possible risk with paternal exposure under review — inform male patients.
- Hyperammonemia (with/without encephalopathy), thrombocytopenia, weight gain, tremor, hair loss.

## Major interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| **Carbapenems** (meropenem, ertapenem) | Valproate levels fall sharply → seizures — avoid |
| Lamotrigine | Lamotrigine levels ~double → serious rash (SJS/TEN) risk — reduce lamotrigine dose |
| Enzyme inducers (carbamazepine, phenytoin, rifampicin) | Lower valproate levels |
| Aspirin | Increased free valproate |

## Monitoring
LFTs and full blood count before starting and during the first 6 months; pregnancy test/contraception review for women of childbearing potential.
