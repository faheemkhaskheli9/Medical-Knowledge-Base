# Lamotrigine

**Class / ATC:** Antiseizure medicine (sodium channel blocker), mood stabilizer — N03AX09.

## Indications
Focal and generalized epilepsy (first-line option, including for women of childbearing potential — NICE NG217); maintenance treatment of bipolar I disorder (prevents depressive episodes; not for acute mania).

## Dosing (adult, oral) — **slow titration is mandatory**
Escalation depends on interacting medicines (FDA label, epilepsy and bipolar):

| Co-medication | Weeks 1–2 | Weeks 3–4 | Then |
|---------------|-----------|-----------|------|
| **Valproate** (raises lamotrigine levels) | 25 mg every other day | 25 mg daily | Increase by 25–50 mg/day every 1–2 weeks; usual maintenance 100–200 mg/day with valproate alone |
| Neither valproate nor enzyme inducers | 25 mg daily | 50 mg daily | Increase by 50 mg/day every 1–2 weeks; epilepsy maintenance 225–375 mg/day; bipolar target 200 mg/day |
| **Enzyme inducers** (carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, primidone) without valproate | 50 mg daily | 100 mg/day in 2 divided doses | Increase by 100 mg/day every 1–2 weeks; epilepsy maintenance 300–500 mg/day |

- Restarting after a gap of more than 5 half-lives: re-titrate from the starting dose (rash risk).
- Do not stop abruptly in epilepsy — taper over at least 2 weeks unless a serious rash requires immediate withdrawal.
- Hepatic impairment: reduce doses (label: by about 25% in moderate or severe impairment without ascites, about 50% in severe impairment with ascites). Children: weight-based — use BNFc/label.

## Contraindications
Hypersensitivity to lamotrigine (including previous lamotrigine rash).

## Warnings
- **Serious rash** (Stevens–Johnson syndrome, TEN), FDA boxed warning — mostly within 2–8 weeks of starting. Risk is higher with valproate, exceeding the starting dose or escalation rate, and in children. Stop at the first sign of rash unless clearly unrelated.
- DRESS / multi-organ hypersensitivity; **hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis** (FDA 2018) — fever, rash, lymphadenopathy, cytopenias.
- **Cardiac conduction:** class IB antiarrhythmic activity; may be proarrhythmic in structural heart disease or arrhythmias (FDA 2021) — assess before use in these patients.
- Aseptic meningitis; suicidal thoughts (all antiseizure medicines); blood dyscrasias.

## Interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Valproate | Roughly doubles lamotrigine levels — halve doses and titrate slowly (see table) ([valproate](valproate.md)) |
| Carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, rifampicin, some HIV protease inhibitors (lopinavir/ritonavir, atazanavir/ritonavir) | Lower lamotrigine levels |
| **Estrogen-containing contraceptives** | Lower lamotrigine levels (about 50%); levels rise in the pill-free week — dose adjustment may be needed when starting/stopping |

## Adverse effects
- Common: dizziness, diplopia, ataxia, headache, nausea, insomnia, rash (mostly benign).
- Serious: SJS/TEN, DRESS, HLH, aseptic meningitis.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Among the lower-risk antiseizure options in pregnancy (NICE NG217, MHRA 2021 review). Clearance rises markedly in pregnancy — monitor seizure control and consider levels; reduce after delivery. Folic acid before conception. Passes into breast milk; breastfeeding usually continued with monitoring of the infant (drowsiness, rash, poor feeding). See [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Overdose
Sedation, ataxia, seizures, QRS widening — supportive care, ECG monitoring; contact a poison center.

## Patient counselling
Follow the titration schedule exactly; do not increase faster. Seek urgent help for any rash, fever, mouth ulcers, swollen glands or blistering, especially in the first 8 weeks. If more than a few days' doses are missed, ask before restarting. Tell the prescriber about starting or stopping hormonal contraception. Look-alike name: lamotrigine vs terbinafine (Lamictal/Lamisil) — see [look-alike/sound-alike](../look-alike-sound-alike.md).
