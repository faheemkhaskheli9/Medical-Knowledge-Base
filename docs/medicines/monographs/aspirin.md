# Aspirin (low-dose antiplatelet)

**Class / ATC:** Irreversible COX-1 inhibitor (antiplatelet), salicylate NSAID — B01AC06 (antiplatelet), N02BA01 (analgesic).

## Dosing (adult, oral)
| Indication | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Secondary prevention (coronary disease, ischemic stroke/TIA, peripheral arterial disease) | 75–100 mg once daily (81 mg in the US; 75 mg in the UK) |
| Suspected acute coronary syndrome | 300 mg loading dose, chewed or dispersed (UK); 162–325 mg (US) — then low dose daily |
| Acute ischemic stroke (after hemorrhage excluded by brain imaging) | 300 mg daily for 2 weeks (NICE/RCP), then long-term antiplatelet |
| Pre-eclampsia risk in pregnancy | 75–150 mg once daily from 12 weeks until birth (NICE NG133) |

**Primary prevention** is no longer routine: USPSTF 2022 recommends against starting aspirin at age ≥ 60, and an individual decision at 40–59 years with ≥ 10% 10-year CVD risk, because bleeding risk offsets benefit.

## Contraindications
Children and adolescents (under 16 in the UK) — **Reye's syndrome**, except specialist indications such as Kawasaki disease; active peptic ulceration; hemophilia and other bleeding disorders; previous hypersensitivity to aspirin or other NSAIDs (asthma, angioedema, urticaria). See [pediatrics](../../special-populations/pediatrics.md).

## Warnings
- GI bleeding — higher with age > 65, previous ulcer, anticoagulants, corticosteroids, SSRIs. Consider a PPI for gastroprotection (see [omeprazole](omeprazole.md)).
- Aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (asthma with nasal polyps). See [allergy cross-reactivity](../../allergy/cross-reactivity.md).
- Do not stop after a coronary stent without the cardiologist (stent thrombosis).
- Avoid in dengue (bleeding risk).

## Interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Anticoagulants (warfarin, DOACs), clopidogrel and other antiplatelets | Increased bleeding — combine only when indicated |
| Ibuprofen (regular use) | May block aspirin's antiplatelet effect — FDA: take ibuprofen at least 30 min after or more than 8 h before immediate-release aspirin |
| NSAIDs, corticosteroids, SSRIs/SNRIs | GI bleeding |
| Methotrexate | Reduced methotrexate excretion |

## Adverse effects
- Common: dyspepsia, bruising.
- Serious: GI bleeding or ulceration, hemorrhagic stroke, bronchospasm, angioedema.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Low-dose aspirin is used in pregnancy when indicated (pre-eclampsia prevention). Avoid analgesic (high) doses, especially in the third trimester. Low-dose use is generally considered compatible with breastfeeding; avoid analgesic doses (theoretical Reye's risk).

## Overdose
Salicylate toxicity (tinnitus, vomiting, hyperventilation, metabolic acidosis, confusion). No specific antidote — activated charcoal, urinary alkalinization with sodium bicarbonate, hemodialysis in severe cases. Contact a poison center. See [antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md).

## Patient counselling
Take with or after food. Report black stools, vomiting blood or unusual bleeding. Do not give to children with viral illness. Tell dentists and surgeons. Avoid extra over-the-counter NSAIDs unless advised.
