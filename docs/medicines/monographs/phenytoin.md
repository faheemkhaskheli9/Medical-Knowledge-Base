# Phenytoin

**Class / ATC:** Hydantoin antiepileptic (sodium-channel blocker) — N03AB02. Prodrug for IV/IM use: fosphenytoin. **Narrow therapeutic index with non-linear (saturable) kinetics:** small dose increases can cause large rises in serum level. Enzyme inducer and highly protein bound (about 90%, mainly to albumin).

## Dosing (adult)
| Route / situation | Dose (FDA) |
|------------|------|
| Oral, no previous treatment | 100 mg extended capsule three times daily; adjust to need (most adults: 100 mg 3–4 times daily); max two 100 mg capsules three times daily |
| Oral once daily | 300 mg once daily may be considered only if seizures are controlled on 100 mg three times daily, using extended capsules |
| Oral loading (clinic or hospital with level monitoring only) | 1 g in three divided doses (400 mg, 300 mg, 300 mg) 2 hours apart; normal maintenance from 24 hours later. Not for patients with renal or liver disease |
| IV loading (status epilepticus) | 10–15 mg/kg at a rate **not exceeding 50 mg/min** in adults, then maintenance every 6–8 hours |

- Use oral phenytoin whenever possible. Follow local status epilepticus protocols for IV loading (many use fosphenytoin).
- Change the dose in small steps; steady state takes 7–10 days, so do not adjust more often than that.
- Different products (capsules, chewable tablets, suspension, phenytoin sodium vs phenytoin base) are **not bioequivalent** — the free acid (base) has about 8% more phenytoin than the same weight of the sodium salt. UK MHRA classes phenytoin as "category 1" (keep the same product). Children: weight-based (label, BNFc).
- **Therapeutic range:** total serum concentration 10–20 mcg/mL; unbound (free) 1–2 mcg/mL. With low albumin, renal failure or pregnancy the total level underestimates free drug — measure a free level where available.

## Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to phenytoin or other hydantoins.
- Coadministration with **delavirdine**.
- IV: sinus bradycardia, sinoatrial block, second- or third-degree AV block, Adams-Stokes syndrome.
- History of prior acute hepatotoxicity attributable to phenytoin (label).

## Warnings / boxed warnings
- **Rapid IV infusion** (boxed): severe hypotension and cardiac arrhythmias; do not exceed 50 mg/min in adults; continuous cardiac and blood pressure monitoring during and after infusion. IV extravasation can cause tissue injury ("purple glove syndrome").
- **SJS/TEN:** HLA-B\*15:02 may increase risk in people of Asian ancestry — consider avoiding phenytoin as an alternative to [carbamazepine](carbamazepine.md) in carriers. CYP2C9 poor metabolizers need lower doses ([pharmacogenomics](../../pharmacogenomics/gene-drug-pairs.md)).
- DRESS / multi-organ hypersensitivity; hepatic injury; blood dyscrasias.
- Suicidal thoughts and behavior (all antiepileptics). Do not stop abruptly.
- Long-term use: gum overgrowth (gingival hyperplasia), coarsening of facial features, hirsutism, reduced bone density (osteomalacia), peripheral neuropathy, cerebellar atrophy.

## Adverse effects
- Common (often dose-related): nystagmus, ataxia, slurred speech, dizziness, drowsiness, nausea, rash, gum overgrowth.
- Serious: SJS/TEN, DRESS, hepatotoxicity, blood dyscrasias, arrhythmia and hypotension (IV), purple glove syndrome.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Increased risk of congenital malformations (fetal hydantoin syndrome) and of neonatal bleeding if used near term (neonates may need vitamin K). Levels fall in pregnancy — monitor (ideally free levels). Plan pregnancy with a specialist; folic acid before conception. Breastfeeding is generally considered acceptable (low infant levels). See [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
Serum level (trough) after dose changes, interacting drugs, suspected toxicity or non-adherence; albumin; full blood count and liver function; rash; ECG and blood pressure during IV loading; gum health; bone health on long-term use.

## Overdose / antidote
Early toxicity: nystagmus, ataxia, slurred speech; severe toxicity (levels above about 30 mcg/mL): lethargy, confusion, coma; arrhythmia mainly with rapid IV dosing. No specific antidote — supportive care and monitoring; contact a poison center ([antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md)).

## Patient counselling points
Take at the same times each day and keep to the same product. Do not stop suddenly. Report unsteadiness, double vision, slurred speech or confusion (possible high level), rash, fever, sore throat or yellowing of the skin. Brush and floss well and see a dentist regularly. Hormonal contraception may be less effective — discuss reliable contraception. Tell every prescriber and pharmacist you take phenytoin.
