# Insulin Types

**ATC:** A10A — **high-alert medication**

## Action profiles (subcutaneous, approximate)
| Type | Examples | Onset | Peak | Duration |
|------|----------|-------|------|----------|
| Rapid-acting | Lispro, aspart, glulisine | ~15 min | 1–2 h | 3–5 h |
| Short-acting (regular) | Human regular insulin | ~30 min | 2–4 h | 6–8 h |
| Intermediate (NPH) | Isophane | 1–2 h | 4–12 h | 12–18 h |
| Long-acting | Glargine U-100, detemir | 1–2 h | Minimal | ~20–24 h |
| Ultra-long-acting | Glargine U-300, degludec | 1–6 h | Minimal | 24–42 h+ |
| Premixed | 70/30, 75/25, 50/50 | Varies | Dual | Up to ~24 h |

## Safety rules
- Always write **"units"** in full — never "U" or "IU" ([unsafe abbreviations](../../clinical-reference/unsafe-abbreviations.md)).
- Standard concentration is **U-100**. Concentrated insulins (U-200, U-300, U-500) — never withdraw from a pen with a syringe; U-500 requires a dedicated U-500 syringe or pen.
- Never share pens or cartridges, even with a new needle.
- Rapid-acting insulin: give within 0–15 minutes before meals (some products at meal start or just after).
- Confirm the product name — HumaLOG/HumuLIN and NovoLOG/NovoLIN are easily confused ([LASA](../look-alike-sound-alike.md)).

## Hypoglycemia
Recognize and treat per [type 2 diabetes](../../conditions/type-2-diabetes.md); patients and families should know glucagon use.

## Storage
Unopened: refrigerate 2–8 °C, do not freeze. In-use pens/vials: room temperature for a limited period (commonly 28 days; varies by product). Protect from heat and sunlight.

## Sick days
Never stop basal insulin in type 1 diabetes; monitor glucose and ketones more often and seek help if ketones are raised or unable to keep fluids down.
