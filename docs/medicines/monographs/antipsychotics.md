# Antipsychotics

**ATC:** N05A. Second-generation: risperidone, olanzapine, quetiapine, aripiprazole, clozapine. First-generation: haloperidol, chlorpromazine.

## Boxed warning
**Increased mortality in older people with dementia-related psychosis** (stroke, death) — avoid for behavioral symptoms of dementia unless severe and other measures fail.

## Key adverse effects by drug (relative)
| Effect | Higher risk | Lower risk |
|--------|-------------|------------|
| Weight gain, diabetes, dyslipidemia | Olanzapine, clozapine, quetiapine | Aripiprazole, haloperidol |
| Extrapyramidal effects, tardive dyskinesia | Haloperidol, high-dose risperidone | Quetiapine, clozapine |
| Hyperprolactinemia | Risperidone, haloperidol | Aripiprazole, quetiapine |
| Sedation | Clozapine, olanzapine, quetiapine | Aripiprazole |
| QT prolongation | Haloperidol (IV), ziprasidone | Aripiprazole |

## Emergencies
- **Neuroleptic malignant syndrome**: fever, severe rigidity, confusion, autonomic instability, raised CK → stop drug, emergency care.
- Acute dystonia (e.g. oculogyric crisis) → anticholinergic (procyclidine/benztropine).

## Metabolic monitoring
Weight/BMI, waist, BP, fasting glucose or HbA1c, lipids at baseline, ~12 weeks, then at least annually; ECG if cardiac risk or QT-prolonging combinations.

## Clozapine (treatment-resistant schizophrenia)
- Mandatory **neutrophil monitoring** (agranulocytosis) per national scheme.
- Myocarditis (early weeks), severe constipation/ileus (can be fatal), seizures, hypersalivation.
- **Smoking changes levels**: stopping smoking raises clozapine levels (CYP1A2) → toxicity; starting smoking lowers them.
