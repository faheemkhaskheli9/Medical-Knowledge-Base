# Opioids (morphine, oxycodone, tramadol, codeine)

**ATC:** N02A — **high-alert medications**

## Starting doses — opioid-naive adults (oral, immediate release)
| Drug | Typical start | Notes |
|------|---------------|-------|
| Morphine | 5–10 mg every 4 h as needed (2.5–5 mg in older/frail) | Avoid in severe renal impairment (active metabolites) |
| Oxycodone | 5 mg every 4–6 h as needed | ~1.5× potency of oral morphine |
| Tramadol | 50–100 mg every 4–6 h | Max 400 mg/day; CrCl < 30: max 200 mg/day, every 12 h |
| Codeine | 30–60 mg every 4–6 h | Max 240 mg/day; avoid in children < 12 and CYP2D6 ultrarapid metabolizers |

Long-acting/extended-release products and **fentanyl patches are not for opioid-naive patients** or acute pain.

## Approximate oral morphine equivalents
| Drug | Dose ≈ 10 mg oral morphine |
|------|----------------------------|
| Oxycodone (oral) | 5–7.5 mg |
| Hydromorphone (oral) | 2 mg |
| Morphine (IV/SC) | 3.3–5 mg |
| Codeine (oral) | 100 mg |
| Tramadol (oral) | 100 mg |

Conversion ratios vary between references. When switching opioids, **reduce the calculated dose by 25–50%** (incomplete cross-tolerance). Methadone and fentanyl conversions need specialist input.

## Boxed warnings / key risks
- Respiratory depression — highest at start and after dose increases.
- **With benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, alcohol, or other CNS depressants:** profound sedation, death.
- Addiction, misuse; neonatal opioid withdrawal syndrome with prolonged use in pregnancy.
- Tramadol: seizures, serotonin syndrome, hypoglycemia, hyponatremia.

## Safe prescribing (CDC 2022)
- Non-opioid therapies first for most pain; immediate-release at the lowest effective dose; short duration for acute pain (often ≤ 3 days, rarely > 7).
- Reassess benefits/risks carefully before increasing to ≥ 50 morphine milligram equivalents (MME)/day.
- **Offer naloxone** when risk factors present (≥ 50 MME/day, benzodiazepines, history of overdose or substance use disorder).
- Do not stop long-term opioids abruptly — taper.

## Side-effect management
Prescribe a laxative (stimulant ± softener) with regular opioids — tolerance to constipation does not develop. Nausea usually settles in days.

## Renal impairment
Avoid morphine and codeine in severe impairment; use cautious doses of oxycodone or hydromorphone; fentanyl/buprenorphine preferred in end-stage disease (specialist advice).
