# Baclofen

**Class / ATC:** Centrally acting skeletal muscle relaxant, GABA-B agonist — M03BX01. Used for spasticity (multiple sclerosis, spinal cord injury). Cleared mainly unchanged by the kidneys.

## Dosing (adult, oral, FDA)
| Population | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Adult, spasticity | Start 5 mg three times daily; increase by 5 mg per dose every 3 days as needed; maximum 80 mg daily (20 mg four times daily) |
| Renal impairment | Use with caution; a lower dose may be necessary (risk of toxicity). Non-US labels suggest low doses (about 5 mg daily) in severe impairment or haemodialysis |
| Older adults | Start low, titrate slowly (sedation, confusion, falls) |

- UK SmPCs use the same starting regimen; some allow higher maximum doses in hospital under close supervision — check the SmPC.
- Intrathecal baclofen (pump) is specialist only; doses are in micrograms, not mg. Never confuse oral and intrathecal products.

## Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to baclofen.
- UK SmPCs also list active peptic ulceration (check the SmPC).

## Warnings / boxed warnings
- **Abrupt withdrawal:** hallucinations and seizures after stopping oral baclofen. Taper slowly unless a serious adverse reaction requires stopping.
- **Intrathecal baclofen — do not discontinue abruptly** (boxed warning): high fever, altered mental status, exaggerated rebound spasticity and muscle rigidity, which in rare cases progresses to rhabdomyolysis, multi-organ failure and death. Causes include pump/catheter failure and missed refills — treat as an emergency.
- **Renal impairment:** baclofen toxicity (encephalopathy, drowsiness, confusion, respiratory depression) can occur at usual doses in CKD. See [renal and hepatic dosing](../../special-populations/renal-hepatic.md).
- Caution in epilepsy (lowers seizure threshold), psychotic disorders, stroke, respiratory impairment.
- Sedation impairs driving; additive with alcohol and CNS depressants.

## Major interactions
| Drug | Effect / action |
|------|-----------------|
| Alcohol, [opioids](opioids.md), benzodiazepines, [Z-drugs](zolpidem.md), [gabapentinoids](gabapentinoids.md) | Additive CNS and respiratory depression |
| Antihypertensives | Additive hypotension |
| Tricyclic antidepressants ([amitriptyline](amitriptyline.md)) | Enhanced muscle relaxation/hypotonia, sedation |
| Anything that reduces renal function (dehydration, acute kidney injury, nephrotoxic drugs such as NSAIDs) | Baclofen accumulation and toxicity |

See [major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md).

## Adverse effects
- Common: drowsiness, dizziness, weakness, fatigue, nausea, confusion.
- Serious: encephalopathy (renal impairment), respiratory depression, seizures and hallucinations on withdrawal, intrathecal withdrawal syndrome.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Specialist advice; neonatal withdrawal reported after use in late pregnancy. See [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
Renal function (eGFR) before starting and if unwell; sedation and mental status; seizure control; for pumps — refill dates, alarms and early withdrawal signs.

## Overdose / antidote
Drowsiness, coma, respiratory depression, hypotonia, seizures, bradycardia or arrhythmia. No specific antidote; supportive care, airway management; haemodialysis may help in renal failure ([antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md)).

## Patient counselling points
Do not stop suddenly — reduce slowly with your prescriber. Avoid alcohol; take care driving. Tell your prescriber about kidney problems or if you become unusually sleepy or confused. If you have a baclofen pump: never miss a refill appointment and seek emergency care for fever, confusion, returning stiffness or itching.
