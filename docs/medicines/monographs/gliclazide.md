# Gliclazide (sulfonylureas)

**Class / ATC:** Sulfonylurea — A10BB09. Others: glimepiride (A10BB12), glipizide (A10BB07), glibenclamide/glyburide (A10BB01). Stimulate insulin release from pancreatic beta cells, so they can cause **hypoglycemia**.

## Dosing (oral, adult)
| Drug | Dose |
|------|------|
| Gliclazide immediate-release (UK) | 40–80 mg daily with breakfast, adjusted to response; up to 160 mg as a single dose. Max 320 mg daily — give doses above 160 mg in 2 divided doses (BNF) |
| Gliclazide modified-release (UK) | 30 mg once daily with breakfast, increased every 4 weeks if needed; max 120 mg daily. MR 30 mg is not interchangeable mg-for-mg with IR 80 mg |
| Glimepiride (US/UK) | 1–2 mg once daily with breakfast; increase gradually; max 8 mg daily (FDA) |
| Glipizide (US) | IR: 5 mg once daily before breakfast (2.5 mg in older adults or liver disease); max 40 mg daily, divide doses above 15 mg. ER: max 20 mg daily (FDA) |

- Older adults, frail patients, renal or hepatic impairment: start at the lowest dose. **Avoid glibenclamide (glyburide)** in older adults (long-acting, prolonged hypoglycemia; Beers).
- Renal impairment: gliclazide is mainly hepatically cleared — use with caution and monitor closely; avoid in severe impairment (BNF/SmPC).

## Indications
Type 2 diabetes when metformin is contraindicated or not tolerated, or as add-on therapy ([type 2 diabetes](../../conditions/type-2-diabetes.md)). Symptomatic hyperglycemia needing a rapid response. Some forms of monogenic diabetes (specialist).

## Contraindications
- Type 1 diabetes, diabetic ketoacidosis.
- Severe hepatic or renal impairment (gliclazide SmPC).
- Hypersensitivity to sulfonylureas or sulfonamides. Pregnancy and breastfeeding (gliclazide SmPC; insulin is preferred).
- Co-administration with miconazole (gliclazide SmPC).

## Warnings
- **Hypoglycemia** — may be severe and prolonged; higher risk with missed meals, alcohol, exercise, older age, renal or hepatic impairment, and interacting drugs. Driving rules apply (e.g. DVLA in the UK).
- **G6PD deficiency:** hemolytic anemia — consider a non-sulfonylurea alternative.
- Weight gain.
- **Sick-day rules:** during illness with poor intake, the dose may need to be reduced or withheld — check glucose more often (UK "SADMANS" includes sulfonylureas).
- Surgery and fasting: follow perioperative diabetes guidance.

## Interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| [Fluconazole](fluconazole.md), miconazole, clarithromycin, co-trimoxazole ([trimethoprim](trimethoprim.md)) | Raise sulfonylurea levels — hypoglycemia |
| Alcohol | Hypoglycemia; disulfiram-like reaction rarely |
| Beta-blockers ([bisoprolol](bisoprolol.md)) | Mask warning signs of hypoglycemia (tremor, palpitations) |
| [Insulin](insulin.md), other glucose-lowering drugs | Additive hypoglycemia — reduce sulfonylurea dose |
| [Corticosteroids](corticosteroids.md), thiazides | Raise glucose — loss of control |
| [Warfarin](warfarin.md) | INR may change — monitor |

## Adverse effects
- Common: hypoglycemia, weight gain, GI upset.
- Serious: severe prolonged hypoglycemia, hemolytic anemia (G6PD), cholestatic jaundice and skin reactions (rare).

## Pregnancy / lactation
Usually switched to insulin before conception or as soon as pregnancy is confirmed (NICE). Gliclazide SmPC: contraindicated in pregnancy and breastfeeding; risk of neonatal hypoglycemia.

## Monitoring
HbA1c every 3–6 months; capillary glucose when risk of hypoglycemia or for drivers; renal and liver function.

## Overdose / antidote
Prolonged, recurrent hypoglycemia. Give **glucose**; **octreotide** reduces recurrent hypoglycemia. Admit and observe — hypoglycemia can recur for many hours ([antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md)).

## Patient counselling
Take with breakfast; do not skip meals. Know the signs of low blood sugar (shaking, sweating, confusion) and carry fast-acting sugar. Check glucose before driving if advised. Limit alcohol. When unwell and not eating, ask about reducing or holding the dose. Tell the prescriber about new medicines, including antifungals and antibiotics.
