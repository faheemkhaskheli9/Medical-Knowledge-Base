# Propranolol

**Class:** Non-selective beta-adrenoceptor blocker. See also [bisoprolol](bisoprolol.md) for shared beta-blocker points.

## Dosing (adult, FDA immediate-release tablets)
| Population | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Hypertension | Start 40 mg PO twice daily (alone or with a diuretic); usual maintenance 120–240 mg per day; some patients may need up to 640 mg per day |
| Angina | 80–320 mg per day, given in 2, 3 or 4 divided doses |
| Migraine prophylaxis | Start 80 mg daily in divided doses; usual effective range 160–240 mg per day. **Not for treating a migraine attack that has already started** |
| Essential tremor | Start 40 mg twice daily; optimum effect usually at 120 mg per day; occasionally 240–320 mg per day |
| Extended-release capsules, paediatric, renal/hepatic impairment, older adults | See label |

## Contraindications
- Cardiogenic shock.
- Sinus bradycardia and greater than first-degree heart block (extended-release labels add sick sinus syndrome, unless a permanent pacemaker is in place).
- **Bronchial asthma.**
- Hypersensitivity to propranolol (extended-release labels).
- Heart failure: wording differs between labels — see label.

## Warnings / boxed warnings
- **Abrupt discontinuation:** worsening angina and myocardial infarction have occurred after abrupt stopping (boxed warning on extended-release capsule labels; Warnings section of tablet labels). Taper over at least a few weeks; if angina worsens after interruption, reinstating propranolol is usually advisable.
- **Diabetes and hypoglycaemia:** may mask warning signs of hypoglycaemia (e.g. tachycardia) and make insulin dose adjustment harder; hypoglycaemia reported after prolonged exertion and in renal insufficiency.
- **Thyrotoxicosis:** may mask signs of hyperthyroidism; abrupt withdrawal may precipitate worsening symptoms, including thyroid storm. Propranolol can alter thyroid function tests.

## Major interactions
| Drug | Effect / action |
|------|-----------------|
| Insulin and other glucose-lowering drugs | Masked hypoglycaemia warning signs — counsel and monitor glucose |
| Other interactions | See label and [major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md) |

## Adverse effects
- Common: see label.
- Serious: worsening angina or myocardial infarction after abrupt withdrawal, masked hypoglycaemia, thyroid storm after abrupt withdrawal in thyrotoxicosis — see label for others.

## Pregnancy / lactation
See label and [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
Heart rate, blood pressure, symptoms of bronchospasm or heart failure; blood glucose in diabetes.

## Overdose / antidote
See label and [antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md).

## Patient counselling points
Do not stop suddenly — your prescriber will reduce the dose gradually. Tell your prescriber if you have asthma or other breathing problems. If you have diabetes, propranolol can hide the usual signs of a low blood sugar (such as a fast heartbeat), so check your glucose. Seek urgent care for wheeze or breathlessness, fainting or very slow pulse.
