# Clarithromycin

**Class / ATC:** Macrolide antibacterial — J01FA09. **Strong CYP3A4 and P-gp inhibitor** — the main safety issue is interactions. Compare [azithromycin](azithromycin.md); see [antimicrobial stewardship](../../infections/antimicrobial-stewardship.md).

## Dosing (adult)
| Indication / population | Dose |
|-------------------------|------|
| Respiratory tract and skin infections (UK SmPC) | 250 mg twice daily; may increase to 500 mg twice daily in severe infections; usual duration 6–14 days (some SmPCs 5–14 days) |
| *Helicobacter pylori* eradication (UK SmPC) | 500 mg twice daily as part of triple therapy with a PPI plus amoxicillin 1000 mg twice daily or metronidazole 400 mg twice daily — follow local guidance |
| Renal impairment, CrCl < 30 mL/min | Reduce dose by **50%** (UK: 250 mg once daily, or 250 mg twice daily in severe infections; do not continue beyond 14 days). FDA: 50% reduction; 75% reduction if also taking atazanavir or ritonavir |

- Base empiric choice on local resistance data.

## Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to macrolides.
- **History of QT prolongation or ventricular arrhythmia, including torsade de pointes.**
- **Colchicine in patients with renal or hepatic impairment** ([colchicine](colchicine.md)).
- **Simvastatin or lovastatin** (rhabdomyolysis) — stop the statin during the course.
- Cisapride, pimozide, ergotamine or dihydroergotamine.
- History of cholestatic jaundice/hepatic dysfunction with prior clarithromycin use (FDA).
- Check the SmPC for further contraindicated combinations (e.g. ticagrelor, some antipsychotics and HIV medicines, depending on product and region).

## Warnings / boxed warnings
- **Patients with heart disease:** FDA 2018 communication — 10-year follow-up of a coronary heart disease trial showed a possible increase in later heart problems and death; consider other antibiotics in patients with heart disease.
- **QT prolongation** — avoid with uncorrected hypokalemia or hypomagnesemia, clinically significant bradycardia, or other QT-prolonging drugs.
- **Hepatotoxicity**, including hepatic failure — stop if hepatitis signs develop.
- ***Clostridioides difficile*-associated diarrhea.**
- Myasthenia gravis exacerbation.
- Do not use in pregnancy unless no alternative (FDA: adverse outcomes in animal studies).

## Major interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Simvastatin, lovastatin | Rhabdomyolysis — contraindicated; [atorvastatin](atorvastatin.md) — use lowest dose or pause |
| [Colchicine](colchicine.md) | Fatal colchicine toxicity — contraindicated with renal/hepatic impairment, otherwise reduce colchicine dose and monitor |
| [Ticagrelor](ticagrelor.md), apixaban, [rivaroxaban](rivaroxaban.md) | Raised levels — bleeding; avoid strong CYP3A inhibitors with ticagrelor |
| [Digoxin](digoxin.md) | Digoxin toxicity (P-gp) — monitor levels |
| [Warfarin](warfarin.md) | Raised INR — monitor |
| Sulfonylureas ([gliclazide](gliclazide.md)), insulin | Hypoglycemia |
| Calcium-channel blockers (verapamil, [amlodipine](amlodipine.md)) | Hypotension, bradyarrhythmia, AKI |
| QT-prolonging drugs ([citalopram](citalopram.md), [antipsychotics](antipsychotics.md), [ondansetron](ondansetron.md)) | Additive QT prolongation |
| [Carbamazepine](carbamazepine.md), tacrolimus, ciclosporin, [tamsulosin](tamsulosin.md), [mirtazapine](mirtazapine.md) | Raised levels of these drugs |

See [major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md).

## Adverse effects
- Common: taste disturbance, nausea, diarrhea, abdominal pain, headache, insomnia.
- Serious: QT prolongation/torsade de pointes, hepatotoxicity, *C. difficile* colitis, severe skin reactions, hypersensitivity.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Avoid in pregnancy unless no alternative. See [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
Full medication review for interactions before prescribing; ECG and electrolytes if QT risk; liver symptoms; INR or digoxin levels where relevant.

## Overdose / antidote
GI symptoms predominate; supportive care with ECG monitoring ([antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md)).

## Patient counselling points
Tell your pharmacist about every medicine you take — clarithromycin interacts with many, including some cholesterol, gout, heart and blood-thinning medicines. Stop simvastatin during the course if told to. A metallic or bitter taste is common. Seek help for palpitations or fainting, yellow skin or eyes, or severe or bloody diarrhea. Finish the course as prescribed.
