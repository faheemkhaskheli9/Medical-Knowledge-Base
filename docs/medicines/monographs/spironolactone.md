# Spironolactone (mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists)

**Class / ATC:** MRA, potassium-sparing diuretic — C03DA01. Other: eplerenone (fewer antiandrogenic effects).

## Dosing (adult, oral, once daily)
| Indication | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Heart failure with reduced EF | 25 mg (12.5 mg if eGFR 30–49 or potassium high-normal); increase to 50 mg after 4–8 weeks if potassium and renal function allow |
| Resistant hypertension | 25 mg if potassium ≤ 4.5 mmol/L (NICE); up to 50 mg |
| Ascites in cirrhosis, primary hyperaldosteronism | Higher doses (100–400 mg/day) under specialist care |

## Contraindications
Hyperkalemia (potassium > 5.0 mmol/L before starting for heart failure); eGFR < 30 for heart failure use; Addison's disease; anuria.

## Warnings
**Hyperkalemia**, especially with ACE inhibitors/ARBs/ARNI, CKD, diabetes and older age; can be fatal. Stop or reduce if potassium > 5.5 mmol/L (ESC).

## Interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| ACE inhibitors, ARBs, sacubitril/valsartan | Hyperkalemia — monitor closely (combination is standard in HFrEF) |
| Potassium supplements, potassium salt substitutes, trimethoprim, amiloride | Hyperkalemia — avoid combination |
| NSAIDs | Hyperkalemia, AKI |
| Digoxin, lithium | Raised levels |

## Adverse effects
Hyperkalemia, raised creatinine, **gynecomastia** and breast pain (dose-related), menstrual irregularity, reduced libido.

## Monitoring
Potassium and creatinine before starting, at 1 and 4 weeks after starting or a dose increase, then at 8 and 12 weeks, 6, 9 and 12 months, then every 4 months (ESC).

## Pregnancy / lactation
Avoid in pregnancy (antiandrogenic effects on a male fetus).

## Patient counselling
Hold during vomiting, diarrhea or dehydration (sick-day rule). Avoid potassium-based salt substitutes. Report breast swelling, muscle weakness or palpitations.
