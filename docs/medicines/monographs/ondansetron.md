# Ondansetron

**Class / ATC:** 5-HT3 receptor antagonist (antiemetic) — A04AA01. Others: granisetron, palonosetron.

## Dosing (adult)
| Indication | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Chemotherapy-induced nausea/vomiting (moderately emetogenic), oral | 8 mg 30 min before chemotherapy, 8 mg 8 h later, then 8 mg every 12 h for 1–2 days after (FDA) |
| Postoperative nausea/vomiting prevention | 4 mg IV immediately before induction of anesthesia (or postoperatively), or 16 mg PO 1 h before induction (FDA) |
| Any IV dose | **Single IV dose must not exceed 16 mg** (QT prolongation; FDA 2012) |
| Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C) | Max 8 mg/day total |

Children: weight- or body-surface-based doses — use BNFc or the label. Use for vomiting in childhood gastroenteritis (to support oral rehydration) is common but off-label in some countries.

## Contraindications
Concomitant **apomorphine** (profound hypotension and loss of consciousness); hypersensitivity to ondansetron or other 5-HT3 antagonists. Avoid in congenital long QT syndrome.

## Warnings
- **QT prolongation, torsades de pointes** — dose-dependent. Correct hypokalemia and hypomagnesemia first; ECG monitoring with heart failure, bradyarrhythmia or other QT-prolonging drugs.
- **Serotonin syndrome** with SSRIs, SNRIs, tramadol, MAOIs, lithium and other serotonergic drugs.
- Masks progressive ileus or gastric distension — assess after abdominal surgery.
- Not a substitute for diagnosing the cause of vomiting — check [red flags](../../guidelines/red-flags.md) (e.g. bowel obstruction, raised intracranial pressure, DKA).

## Interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Apomorphine | Severe hypotension — contraindicated |
| Other QT-prolonging drugs (macrolides, fluoroquinolones, antipsychotics, methadone, citalopram) | Torsades risk ([major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md)) |
| Serotonergic drugs | Serotonin syndrome |
| Strong CYP3A4 inducers (rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin) | Reduced ondansetron effect |

## Adverse effects
- Common: headache, constipation, fatigue, flushing or warm sensation.
- Serious: QT prolongation/arrhythmia, hypersensitivity reactions, serotonin syndrome.

## Pregnancy / lactation
First-trimester use is linked to a small increased risk of orofacial clefts (MHRA 2019: about 3 extra cases per 10,000 pregnancies); not first-line for nausea and vomiting of pregnancy — specialist guidance, discuss risks and benefits. Limited breastfeeding data.

## Patient counselling
May cause constipation and headache. Seek urgent help for palpitations, fainting, or agitation with fever, sweating and muscle twitching. Seek medical review if vomiting persists, contains blood, or comes with severe abdominal pain or dehydration.
