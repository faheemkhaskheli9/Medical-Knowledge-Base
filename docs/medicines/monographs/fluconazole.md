# Fluconazole

**Class / ATC:** Triazole antifungal — J02AC01. Strong inhibitor of CYP2C19 and moderate inhibitor of CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 (FDA). Long half-life (about 30 hours), so interactions can persist for several days after stopping.

## Dosing (adult)
| Indication | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Vaginal candidiasis (uncomplicated) | 150 mg PO single dose |
| Oropharyngeal candidiasis | 200 mg PO on day 1, then 100 mg once daily for at least 2 weeks (FDA) |
| Esophageal candidiasis | 200 mg on day 1, then 100 mg once daily (up to 400 mg/day) for at least 3 weeks and 2 weeks after symptoms resolve (FDA) |
| Renal impairment (CrCl ≤ 50 mL/min, multiple doses) | Normal loading dose, then 50% of the usual daily dose (FDA). Hemodialysis: full dose after each session. No adjustment for a single 150 mg dose |

Serious systemic infections (candidemia, cryptococcal meningitis) need specialist dosing per IDSA guidance. Children: weight-based doses — use BNFc or the label.

## Contraindications
- Co-administration with QT-prolonging drugs metabolised by CYP3A4: **pimozide, quinidine, erythromycin**, cisapride (FDA).
- Hypersensitivity to fluconazole or other azoles.

## Warnings
- **Hepatotoxicity** (rarely fatal) — monitor LFTs with prolonged use; stop if liver injury develops.
- **QT prolongation and torsades de pointes** — caution with other QT-prolonging drugs, hypokalemia, structural heart disease.
- Anaphylaxis and severe skin reactions (SJS/TEN, DRESS).

## Interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| **Warfarin** | Raised INR, bleeding — monitor INR ([warfarin](warfarin.md), [major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md)) |
| Phenytoin | Raised phenytoin levels — monitor |
| Sulfonylureas (glipizide, glimepiride, gliclazide) | Hypoglycemia |
| Simvastatin, atorvastatin | Myopathy risk ([atorvastatin](atorvastatin.md)) |
| Ciclosporin, tacrolimus, sirolimus | Raised levels — monitor drug levels |
| Clopidogrel | Reduced activation (CYP2C19 inhibition) ([clopidogrel](clopidogrel.md)) |
| Other QT-prolonging drugs (citalopram, methadone, macrolides, antipsychotics) | Torsades risk |
| Rifampicin | Reduced fluconazole levels |

## Adverse effects
- Common: headache, nausea, abdominal pain, diarrhea, rash, raised liver enzymes.
- Serious: hepatotoxicity, QT prolongation, severe skin reactions.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Prolonged high doses (400–800 mg/day) in the first trimester are linked to a pattern of birth defects (FDA 2011). For vaginal candidiasis in pregnancy, CDC recommends topical azoles (7 days) rather than oral fluconazole. Excreted in breast milk at levels below doses given to infants; considered acceptable during breastfeeding (LactMed).

## Overdose
Hallucinations and paranoid behaviour reported. Supportive care; removed by hemodialysis. See [antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md).

## Patient counselling
Tell your pharmacist about all other medicines — fluconazole interacts with blood thinners, diabetes tablets, statins and heart medicines, and the effect lasts a few days after a single dose. Seek advice if symptoms do not improve within a few days, or for recurrent thrush (4 or more episodes a year). Report yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, palpitations or fainting.
