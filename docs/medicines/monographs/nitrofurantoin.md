# Nitrofurantoin

**Class / ATC:** Urinary antibacterial — J01XE01. WHO AWaRe **Access** group.

## Dosing (adult, oral)
| Indication | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Uncomplicated cystitis, non-pregnant women | 100 mg modified-release twice daily for 3 days (NICE) or 5 days (IDSA) |
| Cystitis in men, or in pregnancy (not at term) | 100 mg modified-release twice daily for 7 days |

Take with food. Use only for **lower** UTI — it does not reach therapeutic levels in kidney or blood, so **not for pyelonephritis or urosepsis**.

## Renal impairment
Avoid if eGFR < 45 mL/min/1.73 m². A short course (3–7 days) may be used with caution at eGFR 30–44 only for a resistant organism when benefit outweighs risk (MHRA 2014).

## Contraindications
eGFR < 45 (as above); G6PD deficiency (hemolysis); pregnancy at term (38–42 weeks), during labour, and infants under 3 months (neonatal hemolysis).

## Adverse effects
- Common: nausea, headache, brown urine (harmless)
- Serious: **acute pulmonary reaction** (fever, cough, dyspnea within days); **chronic pulmonary fibrosis, hepatitis and peripheral neuropathy** with long-term use, older age or renal impairment

## Interactions
Magnesium trisilicate antacids reduce absorption. Probenecid reduces urinary excretion (less effect, more toxicity).

## Monitoring
For long-term prophylaxis: lung symptoms, liver function and renal function periodically; stop if pulmonary, hepatic or neurological symptoms develop.

## Patient counselling
Finish the course. Seek help for fever, back pain, vomiting (possible kidney infection) or breathlessness.
