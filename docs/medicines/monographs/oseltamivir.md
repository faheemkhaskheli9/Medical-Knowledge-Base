# Oseltamivir

**Class:** Antiviral — influenza neuraminidase inhibitor (influenza A and B). Not active against COVID-19, RSV or the common cold. Does not replace annual [influenza vaccination](../../vaccination/vaccination.md).

## Dosing (adults and adolescents 13 years and older, FDA label)
| Use | Dose |
|-----|------|
| **Treatment** | **75 mg PO twice daily for 5 days**, started within 48 hours of symptom onset (label) |
| **Post-exposure prophylaxis** | **75 mg PO once daily for at least 10 days** after close contact, started within 48 hours of exposure |
| Community outbreak prophylaxis | 75 mg PO once daily for up to 6 weeks |

| Renal function (creatinine clearance) | Treatment | Prophylaxis |
|--------------------------------------|-----------|-------------|
| More than 60 mL/min | 75 mg twice daily | 75 mg once daily |
| More than 30 to 60 mL/min | 30 mg twice daily | 30 mg once daily |
| More than 10 to 30 mL/min | 30 mg once daily | 30 mg every other day |
| Haemodialysis, CAPD | See label (dosed around dialysis sessions) | See label |
| End-stage renal disease not on dialysis | **Not recommended** | Not recommended |

- Paediatric doses are weight-based and the oral suspension strength matters — see label; dosing errors with mL vs mg have occurred.
- Hepatic impairment: no adjustment in mild to moderate impairment; severe impairment not studied — see label.
- National guidance may recommend treatment beyond 48 hours from onset for hospitalised, severely ill or high-risk patients — check current local guidance.

## Contraindications
- Known serious hypersensitivity to oseltamivir or any component of the product.

## Warnings / boxed warnings
- **Serious skin and hypersensitivity reactions** (anaphylaxis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, erythema multiforme): stop and treat.
- **Neuropsychiatric events:** delirium, abnormal behaviour and self-injury have been reported in patients with influenza on oseltamivir, mainly children; influenza itself can cause these. Monitor for abnormal behaviour.
- **Bacterial infections:** serious bacterial infections may begin with influenza-like symptoms or complicate influenza; oseltamivir does not prevent them.

## Major interactions
| Drug | Effect / action |
|------|-----------------|
| Live attenuated influenza vaccine (intranasal) | Avoid LAIV within 2 weeks before or 48 hours after oseltamivir, unless medically indicated (antiviral may reduce vaccine response). Inactivated vaccines are not affected |
| Others | No clinically significant CYP interactions expected — see label |

See [major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md).

## Adverse effects
- Common: nausea, vomiting (taking with food may improve tolerability), headache — see label.
- Serious: skin and hypersensitivity reactions, neuropsychiatric events (see warnings).

## Pregnancy / lactation
See label and current national influenza guidance (several recommend antiviral treatment for pregnant patients with influenza) and [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
Renal function to set the dose; clinical response and red flags for severe influenza (breathlessness, chest pain, confusion — see [red flags](../../guidelines/red-flags.md)); behaviour changes, especially in children.

## Overdose / antidote
No specific antidote; supportive care — see label and [antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md).

## Patient counselling points
Start as soon as possible after flu symptoms begin. Take the full course (usually twice a day for 5 days for treatment); taking it with food can reduce nausea. It does not replace the flu vaccine. Seek urgent care for difficulty breathing, chest pain, confusion, or symptoms that improve then get worse. Watch children for unusual behaviour. Stop and seek help for rash with blistering or swelling of the face or throat.
