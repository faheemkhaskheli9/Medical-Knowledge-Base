# Hydralazine

**Class:** Direct-acting arteriolar vasodilator. Not first-line for uncomplicated hypertension — see [hypertension](../../conditions/hypertension.md). Usually given with a beta-blocker (to blunt reflex tachycardia) and a diuretic (to counter fluid retention).

## Dosing (adult, FDA hydralazine tablets, hypertension)
| Population | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Adult (oral) | Start **10 mg PO four times daily for the first 2 to 4 days**, then 25 mg four times daily for the rest of the first week; from the second week, 50 mg four times daily if needed. Use the lowest effective dose |
| Higher doses | Lupus-like syndrome risk rises with dose and duration — see label for higher-dose limits |
| Heart failure (with isosorbide dinitrate) | Fixed-dose combination (BiDil) has its own titration — see that label |
| IV/IM (e.g. severe hypertension, including in pregnancy) | Specialist use per local protocol — see label |
| Paediatric, renal/hepatic impairment, older adults | See label |

- UK labeling uses a lower maximum and asks for acetylator status before higher doses — see the SmPC.

## Contraindications
- **US label:** hypersensitivity to hydralazine; **coronary artery disease**; **mitral valvular rheumatic heart disease**.
- **UK SmPC (additional):** idiopathic systemic lupus erythematosus and related diseases, severe tachycardia, high-output heart failure, heart failure due to mechanical obstruction (e.g. aortic or mitral stenosis, constrictive pericarditis), isolated right heart failure due to pulmonary hypertension, dissecting aortic aneurysm, acute porphyria — see SmPC.

## Warnings / boxed warnings
- **Drug-induced lupus-like syndrome** (including glomerulonephritis): more likely with higher doses, longer treatment, in slow acetylators and in women. Check ANA and full blood count if symptoms appear (arthralgia, fever, rash, chest pain); stop unless benefit clearly outweighs risk.
- **Myocardial ischaemia:** reflex tachycardia can provoke angina, ECG ischaemic changes and MI — use with caution in suspected coronary disease (contraindicated in the US label).
- Peripheral neuritis (paraesthesia) — pyridoxine has been used; blood dyscrasias — see label.
- Postural hypotension; use caution in cerebrovascular disease and advanced renal impairment.

## Major interactions
| Drug | Effect / action |
|------|-----------------|
| Other antihypertensives, nitrates ([isosorbide mononitrate](isosorbide-mononitrate.md)), diuretics | Additive hypotension (combinations are often intended — titrate carefully) |
| Beta-blockers ([propranolol](propranolol.md), [bisoprolol](bisoprolol.md)) | Used together intentionally to limit reflex tachycardia; hydralazine may raise propranolol/metoprolol levels — see label |
| MAOIs | Use with caution — see label |
| Diazoxide (IV) | Profound hypotension reported — see label |

See [major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md).

## Adverse effects
- Common: headache, palpitations, tachycardia, flushing, nausea, anorexia, diarrhoea — see label.
- Serious: lupus-like syndrome, angina/MI, blood dyscrasias, peripheral neuritis — see label.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Used in pregnancy for severe hypertension under specialist care — see label, local obstetric guidance and [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
Blood pressure and heart rate; symptoms of lupus (consider ANA and full blood count during long-term or higher-dose use); fluid retention; angina symptoms.

## Overdose / antidote
Hypotension, tachycardia, myocardial ischaemia, arrhythmia, shock — emergency care; no specific antidote; supportive care — see label and [antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md).

## Patient counselling points
Take regularly as prescribed, usually with other blood-pressure medicines. Get up slowly if you feel dizzy. Tell your doctor about joint pain, fever, rash or chest pain on breathing (possible lupus-like reaction), or tingling in the hands or feet. Seek urgent care for chest pain or a racing heartbeat.
