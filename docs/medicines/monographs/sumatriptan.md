# Sumatriptan (triptans)

**Class / ATC:** Selective 5-HT1B/1D receptor agonist (triptan) — N02CC01. Others: rizatriptan, zolmitriptan, naratriptan, eletriptan, almotriptan, frovatriptan. For **acute** treatment of migraine (with or without aura) and cluster headache (injection, nasal); not for prevention.

## Dosing (adult)
| Route | Dose |
|-------|------|
| Oral | US: 25, 50 or 100 mg; may repeat after at least 2 hours; max 200 mg in 24 hours (FDA). UK: 50 mg (up to 100 mg); may repeat after at least 2 hours if migraine recurs; max 300 mg in 24 hours (BNF) |
| Subcutaneous | 6 mg; may repeat once after at least 1 hour; max 12 mg in 24 hours |
| Nasal spray | US: 5, 10 or 20 mg; may repeat after 2 hours; max 40 mg in 24 hours (FDA). UK: 10–20 mg (BNF) |
| Mild to moderate hepatic impairment | Oral: max single dose 50 mg (FDA). Severe impairment: contraindicated |

- Take at the start of the headache phase. If the **first dose does not help**, do not take a second dose for the same attack (UK SmPC).
- NICE CG150: offer a triptan with an NSAID or paracetamol for acute migraine; nasal triptan for ages 12–17.

## Contraindications
- **Ischemic heart disease**, coronary vasospasm (including Prinzmetal angina), previous MI.
- **History of stroke or TIA**; peripheral vascular disease; ischemic bowel disease.
- **Uncontrolled hypertension.**
- Hemiplegic or basilar migraine (FDA).
- Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome or other accessory-pathway arrhythmias (FDA).
- Within 24 hours of an **ergotamine** (or dihydroergotamine) or another triptan.
- MAO-A inhibitor use within the past 2 weeks (oral and nasal, FDA).
- Severe hepatic impairment. Hypersensitivity.

## Warnings
- Chest, throat, neck or jaw tightness or pressure is common and usually not cardiac — but MI and arrhythmias have occurred. Assess cardiovascular risk before first use; consider giving the first dose under supervision with an ECG in people with risk factors (FDA).
- **Serotonin syndrome** reported with SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs and MAOIs.
- **Medication overuse headache:** using triptans on 10 or more days a month can worsen headaches (NICE).
- Seizures (rare). Raised blood pressure.
- A sudden severe ("thunderclap") headache, headache with fever and neck stiffness, or new neurological deficits is **not** typical migraine — urgent assessment ([red flags](../../guidelines/red-flags.md)).

## Interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Ergotamine, other triptans | Additive vasospasm — separate by 24 hours |
| MAO-A inhibitors | Raise sumatriptan levels — contraindicated |
| SSRIs ([sertraline](sertraline.md)), SNRIs, [amitriptyline](amitriptyline.md), lithium | Serotonin syndrome risk — monitor |

## Adverse effects
- Common: tingling, warmth or flushing, chest or throat tightness, dizziness, drowsiness, nausea, injection-site reactions; taste disturbance (nasal).
- Serious: myocardial ischemia, arrhythmia, stroke, serotonin syndrome, hypersensitivity.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Pregnancy registry data have not shown a clear increase in major birth defects; use when benefit outweighs risk, with specialist advice. Passes into breast milk in small amounts; LactMed considers it acceptable. UK SmPC: infant exposure can be minimised by avoiding breastfeeding for 12 hours after a dose.

## Overdose
Expect exaggerated adverse effects; monitor for at least 10 hours with ECG. Supportive care. See [antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md).

## Patient counselling
Take when the headache starts (not during the aura before it). You can take a second dose after 2 hours if the migraine comes back — not if the first dose did not work. Do not exceed the daily maximum, and keep painkiller and triptan use to fewer than 10 days a month. Seek urgent help for chest pain that does not settle, sudden severe headache, weakness, or trouble speaking.
