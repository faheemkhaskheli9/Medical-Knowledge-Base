# Hydroxyzine

**Class:** First-generation (sedating) H1 antihistamine; also used as an anxiolytic. See also [cetirizine](cetirizine.md) (a hydroxyzine metabolite) for non-sedating antihistamines.

## Dosing
| Population | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Adult, anxiety (US label) | 50–100 mg PO four times daily |
| Adult, pruritus (US label) | 25 mg three times daily (see label) |
| Child under 6 years, anxiety (US label) | 50 mg daily in divided doses |
| Child over 6 years, anxiety (US label) | 50–100 mg daily in divided doses |
| **UK (MHRA 2015)** — adult maximum | **100 mg per day** (also children over 40 kg) |
| **UK (MHRA 2015)** — older adults | Avoid; if use cannot be avoided, maximum **50 mg per day** |
| **UK (MHRA 2015)** — children up to 40 kg | Maximum 2 mg/kg per day |
| Renal/hepatic impairment | See label |

- **Regional difference:** the US label's anxiety dose (up to 400 mg/day) exceeds the UK maximum of 100 mg/day, which was set because of QT risk. Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest time.

## Contraindications
- **Early pregnancy** (US label: human data inadequate to establish safety).
- **Prolonged QT interval** (US labels; MHRA: do not prescribe to people with a prolonged QT interval or risk factors for QT prolongation).
- Other contraindications (e.g. hypersensitivity): see label; the UK cetirizine SmPC lists hydroxyzine hypersensitivity — see [cetirizine](cetirizine.md).

## Warnings / boxed warnings
- **QT prolongation and torsade de pointes:** reported post-marketing, mostly in patients with other risk factors (cardiovascular disease, significant bradycardia, electrolyte imbalance, other QT-prolonging drugs).
- **Older adults** are more susceptible to hydroxyzine's side effects (MHRA) — avoid where possible; see [older adults](../../special-populations/older-adults.md).

## Major interactions
| Drug | Effect / action |
|------|-----------------|
| QT-prolonging drugs (e.g. class IA and III antiarrhythmics, some antipsychotics, [citalopram](citalopram.md)/escitalopram) | Additive QT risk — avoid or weigh risk (UK SmPC) |
| Drugs that lower heart rate or potassium | Increased torsade risk — weigh risk (MHRA) |
| Other interactions | See label |

See [major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md).

## Adverse effects
- Common: see label.
- Serious: QT prolongation, torsade de pointes.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Contraindicated in early pregnancy (US label). Otherwise see label and [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
ECG in patients with QT risk factors; sedation and confusion in older adults.

## Overdose / antidote
Overdose may cause QT prolongation and torsade de pointes — ECG monitoring is recommended. No specific antidote; supportive care — see label and [antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md).

## Patient counselling points
Do not drive or use machinery if you feel drowsy. Do not exceed the dose on your label. Tell your prescriber about heart rhythm problems, fainting, or other medicines for heart rhythm, mood or psychosis. Seek urgent care for palpitations, fainting or a racing/irregular heartbeat.
