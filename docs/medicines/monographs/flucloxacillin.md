# Flucloxacillin

**Class / ATC:** Penicillinase-resistant (anti-staphylococcal) penicillin — J01CF05. Used for *Staphylococcus aureus* (not MRSA) and streptococcal skin and soft-tissue infections, and others per local guidance. See [antimicrobial stewardship](../../infections/antimicrobial-stewardship.md); compare [amoxicillin](amoxicillin.md), [cefalexin](cefalexin.md).

## Dosing (adult, UK SmPC)
| Route / population | Dose |
|--------------------|------|
| Oral, adult | 250 mg four times daily; in serious infections the dose may be doubled (500 mg four times daily) |
| IV/IM, children, and severe infections (e.g. osteomyelitis, endocarditis) | Use the SmPC and local guidance — higher, weight-based or specialist regimens |
| Hepatic impairment | No dose reduction needed, but caution (higher risk of hepatic reactions) |
| Severe renal impairment | Consider dose reduction or longer interval (check SmPC) |

- Take oral doses on an empty stomach — 1 hour before food or 2 hours after.
- Duration by indication, per local guidance (e.g. NICE cellulitis guidance).

## Contraindications
- Penicillin hypersensitivity (cross-reactivity with other beta-lactams — see [allergy cross-reactivity](../../allergy/cross-reactivity.md)).
- Previous flucloxacillin-associated jaundice or hepatic dysfunction.

## Warnings / boxed warnings
- **Hepatitis and cholestatic jaundice:** not dose- or route-related; onset may be **delayed up to 2 months after treatment**; rarely fatal. Higher risk in people over 50 years, with hepatic dysfunction or serious underlying disease, and with prolonged courses (more than 14 days).
- **High anion gap metabolic acidosis (pyroglutamic/5-oxoproline acidosis) with paracetamol:** EU SmPC warning — caution with concomitant [paracetamol](paracetamol.md), especially in severe renal impairment, sepsis or malnutrition and at maximum paracetamol doses. Monitor acid-base status (urinary 5-oxoproline); acidosis may persist if flucloxacillin continues after paracetamol stops.
- **Severe cutaneous reactions** and anaphylaxis; ***C. difficile*-associated diarrhoea.**
- Hypokalaemia reported with high IV doses; sodium load in IV preparations.

## Major interactions
| Drug | Effect / action |
|------|-----------------|
| [Paracetamol](paracetamol.md) | High anion gap metabolic acidosis in at-risk patients — monitor |
| Methotrexate ([weekly methotrexate](methotrexate.md)) | Reduced methotrexate excretion — toxicity |
| Probenecid | Higher flucloxacillin levels |
| Voriconazole | Flucloxacillin may lower voriconazole levels — monitor |
| [Warfarin](warfarin.md) | INR changes reported with penicillins — monitor |

See [major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md).

## Adverse effects
- Common: nausea, diarrhoea, rash.
- Serious: anaphylaxis, cholestatic hepatitis (may be delayed), SJS/TEN, *C. difficile* colitis, metabolic acidosis with paracetamol, blood dyscrasias.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Penicillins are generally considered acceptable in pregnancy and breastfeeding when indicated. See [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
Clinical response; liver function in prolonged courses or risk factors; acid-base status if unwell and on regular paracetamol; renal function for high IV doses.

## Overdose / antidote
Mainly GI upset; very high IV doses may cause neurotoxicity (seizures), especially in renal impairment. Supportive care ([antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md)).

## Patient counselling points
Take on an empty stomach, four times a day, and finish the course as prescribed. Tell your doctor if you have had a penicillin allergy. Seek medical help for yellow skin or eyes, dark urine or pale stools — even weeks after finishing the course — and urgently for swelling of the face or breathing difficulty, or a blistering rash.
