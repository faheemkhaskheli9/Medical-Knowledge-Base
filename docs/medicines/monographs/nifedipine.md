# Nifedipine

**Class:** Dihydropyridine calcium-channel blocker. See also [amlodipine](amlodipine.md); for rate-limiting calcium-channel blockers see [diltiazem](diltiazem.md).

## Dosing (adult, FDA)
| Population | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Hypertension or angina (Procardia XL extended-release) | Start 30 mg or 60 mg PO once daily; titration above 120 mg/day not recommended |
| Extended-release tablets | **Swallow whole — do not bite or divide** |
| Immediate-release capsules | **Not for acute reduction of blood pressure** (see warnings); dosing — see label |
| With CYP3A inhibitors (e.g. fluconazole, itraconazole, clarithromycin, erythromycin) | Consider starting at the lowest available dose |
| Paediatric, hepatic impairment, older adults, other brands (e.g. Adalat CC) | See label |

## Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to nifedipine.
- **Strong CYP3A inducers such as rifampicin (rifampin):** contraindicated in some generic extended-release labels (efficacy may be significantly reduced); not listed in the Procardia XL label — check the product label.

## Warnings / boxed warnings
- **Immediate-release nifedipine must not be used for acute blood pressure lowering** (oral or sublingual): profound hypotension, myocardial infarction and death have been reported. Immediate-release nifedipine showed no benefit, and sometimes worse outcomes, in acute myocardial infarction trials.
- **Excessive hypotension:** usually during initial titration or dose increases; may be more common with a beta-blocker.
- **With beta-blockers:** occasional reports of heart failure, severe hypotension or worsening angina — monitor blood pressure closely.
- **GI obstruction (extended-release tablets):** rare obstructive symptoms in patients with strictures; bezoars; serious obstruction also reported without known GI disease. Risk factors include severe GI narrowing, colon cancer, small bowel obstruction, bowel resection and gastric retention. Tablet adherence to the GI wall with ulceration reported.
- **Peripheral oedema:** in patients with heart failure, distinguish it from worsening left ventricular dysfunction.

## Major interactions
| Drug | Effect / action |
|------|-----------------|
| CYP3A inhibitors (fluconazole, itraconazole, [clarithromycin](clarithromycin.md), erythromycin) | Higher nifedipine exposure — start at the lowest dose, monitor blood pressure |
| Strong CYP3A inducers (rifampicin) | Loss of efficacy — contraindicated in some labels |
| Beta-blockers | Hypotension, heart failure, angina — monitor |
| Other interactions (including grapefruit) | See label and [major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md) |

## Adverse effects
- Common: see label (peripheral oedema is described in the label).
- Serious: severe hypotension, worsening angina, GI obstruction (extended-release).

## Pregnancy / lactation
See label, local guidance and [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
Blood pressure (especially during titration and with beta-blockers); heart rate; oedema; interacting medicines.

## Overdose / antidote
See label and [antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md).

## Patient counselling points
Swallow extended-release tablets whole; do not chew, bite or split them. Do not switch between nifedipine products without advice from your prescriber or pharmacist. Tell your doctor if you have had bowel narrowing or surgery. Seek urgent care for chest pain, fainting, severe dizziness or abdominal pain with vomiting or no bowel movements.
