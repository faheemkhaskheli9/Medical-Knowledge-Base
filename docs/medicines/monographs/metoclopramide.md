# Metoclopramide

**Class / ATC:** Dopamine D2 antagonist, prokinetic antiemetic — A03FA01.

## Dosing (adult)
| Setting | Dose |
|---------|------|
| EU / UK (nausea and vomiting, including postoperative, migraine-associated, radiotherapy/chemotherapy-induced) | 10 mg PO, IM or IV up to 3 times daily; **max 30 mg (or 0.5 mg/kg) in 24 hours; max 5 days** (EMA/MHRA 2013) |
| EU / UK body weight under 60 kg | 0.1–0.15 mg/kg up to 3 times daily (EMA 2013) |
| US — diabetic gastroparesis | 10 mg PO up to 4 times daily, 30 minutes before meals and at bedtime, for 2–8 weeks (FDA) |
| US — symptomatic gastroesophageal reflux | 10–15 mg PO up to 4 times daily for 4–12 weeks (FDA) |
| IV administration | Slow bolus over at least 3 minutes (EMA/MHRA) |

- **Do not use for longer than 12 weeks** (FDA boxed warning).
- Lower doses in older adults, renal or hepatic impairment and CYP2D6 poor metabolisers — use the label or BNF.
- Children: EU/UK — contraindicated under 1 year; ages 1–18 only second-line for delayed chemotherapy-induced or postoperative nausea and vomiting (EMA 2013). Use BNFc.

## Contraindications
- Where increased GI motility could be harmful: GI hemorrhage, mechanical obstruction, perforation; within 3–4 days of GI surgery (EU/UK).
- **Pheochromocytoma** (hypertensive crisis).
- Epilepsy (FDA); history of tardive dyskinesia or a dystonic reaction to metoclopramide.
- EU/UK also: Parkinson's disease; use with levodopa or dopamine agonists; history of methemoglobinemia with metoclopramide or NADH-cytochrome b5 reductase deficiency; children under 1 year.

## Warnings
- **Boxed warning (FDA) — tardive dyskinesia:** often irreversible; risk rises with duration and total dose, and in older adults (especially women). Stop if signs appear.
- **Acute dystonic reactions** (oculogyric crisis, torticollis), more common in children and young adults, usually within days of starting.
- Neuroleptic malignant syndrome; parkinsonism; akathisia; depression and suicidal ideation.
- Hyperprolactinemia; cardiac conduction disorders, particularly with IV use (EU SmPC).

## Interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Levodopa, dopamine agonists | Mutual antagonism — contraindicated in EU/UK |
| [Antipsychotics](antipsychotics.md) | Extrapyramidal effects, NMS |
| Strong CYP2D6 inhibitors (fluoxetine, paroxetine, bupropion, quinidine) | Raised metoclopramide levels — lower dose (FDA) |
| Serotonergic drugs (SSRIs, SNRIs, tramadol) | Serotonin syndrome |
| Alcohol, sedatives, opioids | Additive sedation |

## Adverse effects
- Common: drowsiness, restlessness, fatigue, diarrhea.
- Serious: tardive dyskinesia, acute dystonia, NMS, depression, methemoglobinemia (rare).

## Pregnancy / lactation
Large observational studies have not shown an increase in major malformations with first-trimester use; used for nausea and vomiting of pregnancy when first-line options fail — specialist guidance. Passes into breast milk; short courses are generally considered acceptable ([pregnancy & lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md)).

## Overdose / antidote
Extrapyramidal reactions and acute dystonia respond to an anticholinergic (e.g. procyclidine, benzatropine) or diphenhydramine. Supportive care. See [antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md).

## Patient counselling
Use only for the short time prescribed. Stop and seek advice for involuntary movements (face, tongue, limbs), muscle spasms of the neck or eyes, restlessness, or low mood. Seek urgent help for high fever with stiffness and confusion. May cause drowsiness — avoid driving if affected.
