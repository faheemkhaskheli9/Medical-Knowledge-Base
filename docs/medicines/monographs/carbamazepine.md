# Carbamazepine

**Class / ATC:** Antiepileptic (sodium-channel blocker), dibenzazepine — N03AF01. Related: oxcarbazepine, eslicarbazepine. Uses: focal and generalized tonic-clonic seizures, **trigeminal neuralgia**, bipolar disorder (some extended-release products). Not effective for absence or myoclonic seizures. Potent **enzyme inducer** (CYP3A4 and others) and induces its own metabolism (autoinduction over the first weeks).

## Dosing (oral, FDA tablets)
| Population / indication | Dose | Max |
|------------|------|-----|
| Epilepsy, over 12 years | 200 mg twice daily; increase by up to 200 mg/day at weekly intervals, given 3–4 times daily (twice daily for extended-release) | 1000 mg/day (12–15 years); 1200 mg/day (over 15 years); up to 1600 mg/day in adults in rare instances |
| Epilepsy, 6–12 years | 100 mg twice daily, increased weekly | 1000 mg/day |
| Epilepsy, under 6 years | 10–20 mg/kg/day in 2–3 divided doses (tablets) | Per label |
| Trigeminal neuralgia | 100 mg twice daily, increased gradually until pain controlled | 1200 mg/day |
| Older adults, hepatic or renal impairment | Start low and titrate slowly; check the label and BNF | — |

- Products are **not interchangeable** without care; UK MHRA classes carbamazepine as a "category 1" antiepileptic (keep the same manufacturer's product). Use the BNF for UK doses.
- Serum levels help with adherence, toxicity and interactions; interpret against the local laboratory range.

## Contraindications
- History of bone marrow depression.
- Hypersensitivity to carbamazepine or to tricyclic compounds.
- Use with, or within 14 days of, an **MAO inhibitor**.
- Coadministration with **nefazodone** (label). Coadministration with delavirdine and other NNRTIs that are CYP3A4 substrates (label).

## Warnings / boxed warnings
- **SJS/TEN and HLA-B\*15:02** (boxed): strongly associated in people of Asian ancestry, with risk in some Asian countries about 10 times higher than in mainly white populations. **Screen for HLA-B\*15:02 before starting** in at-risk ancestry; if positive, do not start unless benefit clearly outweighs risk. HLA-A\*31:01 is associated with DRESS and other hypersensitivity reactions. A negative test does not remove the need for vigilance ([pharmacogenomics](../../pharmacogenomics/gene-drug-pairs.md)).
- **Aplastic anemia and agranulocytosis** (boxed): risk 5–8 times that of the general population. Obtain a full blood count before starting; stop if significant bone marrow depression develops.
- DRESS / multi-organ hypersensitivity; cross-reactivity with oxcarbazepine and phenytoin is possible.
- **Hyponatremia** (SIADH), more likely in older adults and with diuretics.
- Suicidal thoughts and behavior (all antiepileptics). Hepatitis. Cardiac conduction disturbances.
- Do not stop abruptly in epilepsy — risk of seizures and status epilepticus.

## Major interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| **Hormonal contraceptives** (combined pill, progestogen-only pill, implant) | Reduced contraceptive efficacy — use a method not affected by enzyme inducers (e.g. copper or levonorgestrel IUD, depot injection) |
| [Warfarin](warfarin.md), DOACs ([apixaban](apixaban.md), [rivaroxaban](rivaroxaban.md)) | Reduced anticoagulant levels — avoid DOACs; monitor INR closely |
| Macrolides (erythromycin, clarithromycin), azoles ([fluconazole](fluconazole.md)), diltiazem, verapamil, fluoxetine, isoniazid, [valproate](valproate.md) | Raise carbamazepine levels — toxicity (dizziness, ataxia, diplopia) |
| Many CYP3A4 substrates (some antiretrovirals, immunosuppressants, [lamotrigine](lamotrigine.md), antipsychotics, statins) | Reduced levels of the other drug |
| Diuretics (thiazides, [furosemide](furosemide.md)) | Symptomatic hyponatremia |

See [major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md).

## Adverse effects
- Common: dizziness, drowsiness, unsteadiness (ataxia), double vision, nausea, vomiting.
- Serious: SJS/TEN, DRESS, aplastic anemia, agranulocytosis, hyponatremia, hepatitis, arrhythmia or AV block.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Associated with major congenital malformations, notably **spina bifida** (lower risk than [valproate](valproate.md)). Plan pregnancy with a specialist; recommend folic acid before conception. Do not stop suddenly. Enzyme induction means hormonal contraception may fail. Breastfeeding: infant levels usually well below the therapeutic range; LactMed considers it compatible, but monitor the infant for sedation, poor feeding and jaundice. See [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
HLA-B\*15:02 (at-risk ancestry) before starting; full blood count, liver function and sodium at baseline and periodically; serum level when indicated; mood and suicidality; rash.

## Overdose / antidote
First signs appear after 1–3 hours; neuromuscular disturbances are most prominent, with seizures, coma and ECG dysrhythmias possible. No specific antidote: supportive care, activated charcoal if early and the airway is protected, ECG monitoring; contact a poison center ([antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md)).

## Patient counselling points
Take regularly and keep to the same brand. Stop and seek urgent help for a rash, blistering, mouth ulcers, fever with swollen glands, sore throat, unusual bruising or bleeding. Hormonal contraceptives may not work — discuss reliable contraception. Tell every prescriber and pharmacist you take carbamazepine because of interactions. Do not stop suddenly; do not drive until seizures are controlled and local rules allow.
