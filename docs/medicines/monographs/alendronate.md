# Alendronate (bisphosphonates)

**Class / ATC:** Oral bisphosphonate; inhibits osteoclast bone resorption — M05BA04. Others: risedronate, ibandronate, IV zoledronic acid.

## Dosing (oral, adult)
| Indication | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Treatment of postmenopausal osteoporosis | 70 mg once weekly, or 10 mg once daily |
| Prevention of postmenopausal osteoporosis (US) | 35 mg once weekly, or 5 mg once daily |
| Osteoporosis in men | 70 mg once weekly, or 10 mg once daily |
| Glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis (US) | 5 mg once daily; 10 mg once daily in postmenopausal women not on estrogen |
| Paget's disease of bone (US) | 40 mg once daily for 6 months |
| **Renal impairment** | CrCl 35–60 mL/min: no adjustment. **CrCl < 35 mL/min: not recommended** (FDA) |

**How to take (FDA):** on waking, with a full glass (180–240 mL) of **plain water only**, at least **30 minutes** before the first food, drink or other medicine of the day. **Stay upright** (sitting or standing) for at least 30 minutes and until after the first food. Do not chew or suck the tablet. Do not take at bedtime.

Correct hypocalcemia and vitamin D deficiency before starting; ensure adequate calcium and vitamin D intake.

## Contraindications
- Abnormalities of the esophagus that delay emptying (stricture, achalasia).
- Inability to stand or sit upright for at least 30 minutes.
- **Hypocalcemia.**
- Hypersensitivity. Oral solution: increased aspiration risk — avoid if at risk.

## Warnings
- **Upper GI injury:** esophagitis, ulcers and erosions — usually from incorrect administration. Stop and seek review for new heartburn, pain on swallowing or retrosternal pain.
- **Osteonecrosis of the jaw (ONJ):** risk higher with cancer, chemotherapy, corticosteroids, poor oral hygiene and invasive dental work. Dental check before starting if risk factors.
- **Atypical femoral fractures** with long-term use — report new thigh, hip or groin pain.
- Osteonecrosis of the external auditory canal (rare).
- Severe bone, joint or muscle pain — consider stopping.
- **Duration:** reassess the need after about 5 years of oral therapy; a drug holiday may be appropriate for lower-risk patients (FDA, ASBMR).

## Interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Calcium, antacids, iron, magnesium, food, coffee, juice | Greatly reduce absorption — take alendronate first and wait at least 30 minutes |
| NSAIDs ([ibuprofen](ibuprofen.md), [aspirin](aspirin.md)) | Increased GI irritation |

## Adverse effects
- Common: abdominal pain, dyspepsia, acid regurgitation, musculoskeletal pain.
- Serious: esophageal ulceration, ONJ, atypical femoral fracture, hypocalcemia.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Not used in pregnancy or breastfeeding. Bisphosphonates persist in bone for years — specialist advice for women who may become pregnant.

## Monitoring
Calcium, vitamin D and renal function at baseline. Dental health. Fracture risk and bone density per local guidance; review at 5 years.

## Overdose
Hypocalcemia, hypophosphatemia, upper GI upset and ulceration. Give milk or antacids to bind the drug; **do not induce vomiting**; keep the patient fully upright. See [antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md).

## Patient counselling
Take once a week on the same day, first thing in the morning with a full glass of plain water, at least 30 minutes before eating, drinking or other medicines. Stay upright for 30 minutes. If you miss the weekly dose, take it the next morning, then return to your usual day — never two tablets on the same day. Tell your dentist you take it. Report heartburn, pain on swallowing, jaw pain or new thigh pain.
