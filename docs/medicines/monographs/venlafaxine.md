# Venlafaxine (SNRIs)

**Class / ATC:** Serotonin-noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor (SNRI) — N06AX16. Others: duloxetine, desvenlafaxine. Uses (Effexor XR, adults): major depressive disorder, generalized anxiety disorder, social anxiety disorder, panic disorder. Compare [sertraline](sertraline.md) (SSRI) and [mirtazapine](mirtazapine.md).

## Dosing (oral, adult)
| Population / product | Dose | Max |
|------------|------|-----|
| Extended-release, MDD (FDA) | Start 37.5–75 mg once daily with food; target 75 mg/day; increase in steps of up to 75 mg/day at intervals of at least 4 days | 225 mg/day |
| Immediate-release (FDA) | 75 mg/day in 2–3 divided doses with food | 225 mg/day in outpatients; up to 375 mg/day in severely depressed inpatients |
| Renal impairment (FDA XR) | Mild to moderate: reduce total daily dose by 25–50%. Dialysis or severe: reduce by 50% or more | — |
| Hepatic impairment (FDA XR) | Child-Pugh A or B: reduce total daily dose by 50%. Child-Pugh C or cirrhosis: reduce by 50% or more | — |
| Children and adolescents | Not approved | — |

UK maximums and titration differ (BNF). Take at about the same time each day; swallow extended-release capsules whole.

## Contraindications
- Use with an **MAO inhibitor**, or within 14 days of stopping one; allow at least 7 days after stopping venlafaxine before starting an MAOI. Also linezolid or IV methylene blue.
- Hypersensitivity.
- UK: avoid in patients at high risk of serious ventricular arrhythmia or with uncontrolled hypertension (BNF/SmPC).

## Warnings / boxed warnings
- **Suicidal thoughts and behaviors** (boxed): increased risk in children, adolescents and young adults; monitor all patients, especially early in treatment and after dose changes ([crisis support](../../mental-health/crisis-support.md)).
- **Raised blood pressure** (dose-related): control hypertension before starting and check blood pressure regularly.
- **Serotonin syndrome**, especially with other serotonergic drugs.
- **Discontinuation syndrome**: dizziness, "electric shock" sensations, nausea, anxiety, irritability, sweating, insomnia. Taper gradually — symptoms are often more marked than with many SSRIs.
- Increased bleeding risk (with NSAIDs, antiplatelets, anticoagulants). **Hyponatremia** (SIADH), especially in older adults and with diuretics.
- Angle-closure glaucoma; activation of mania or hypomania; seizures; interstitial lung disease (rare).
- **Toxicity in overdose** appears higher than with SSRIs (seizures, serotonin toxicity, cardiac effects) — consider the quantity supplied for people at risk of self-harm.

## Major interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| MAOIs, linezolid, methylene blue | Serotonin syndrome — contraindicated |
| Triptans ([sumatriptan](sumatriptan.md)), tramadol ([opioids](opioids.md)), [lithium](lithium.md), [amitriptyline](amitriptyline.md), SSRIs, St John's wort | Serotonin syndrome — monitor or avoid |
| NSAIDs ([ibuprofen](ibuprofen.md)), [aspirin](aspirin.md), [warfarin](warfarin.md), DOACs | Bleeding risk |
| Diuretics ([thiazides](thiazides.md)) | Hyponatremia |
| Strong CYP2D6 or CYP3A4 inhibitors | Raised venlafaxine levels |

## Adverse effects
- Common: nausea, dry mouth, sweating, headache, dizziness, insomnia or drowsiness, constipation, sexual dysfunction, raised blood pressure, tachycardia.
- Serious: serotonin syndrome, hyponatremia, severe hypertension, seizures, bleeding, mania, suicidality.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Third-trimester use may cause poor neonatal adaptation (jitteriness, feeding and breathing problems); SNRIs late in pregnancy may slightly increase postpartum hemorrhage risk. Untreated depression also carries risks — specialist and shared decision. Passes into breast milk; monitor the infant. See [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
Blood pressure (baseline and regularly), mood and suicidality, sodium in older adults or with diuretics, signs of serotonin toxicity when combined with serotonergic drugs.

## Overdose / antidote
Seizures (may be delayed with extended-release), serotonin toxicity, tachycardia, hypotension or hypertension, and cardiac conduction effects with large ingestions. No antidote — supportive care, ECG monitoring, benzodiazepines for seizures and agitation; contact a poison center ([antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md)).

## Patient counselling points
Take with food at the same time each day. It can take 2–4 weeks to feel the benefit. Do not stop suddenly — your prescriber will reduce the dose slowly. Get your blood pressure checked. Seek urgent help for thoughts of self-harm, or for agitation, fever, sweating, shaking and muscle twitching (possible serotonin syndrome). Avoid over-the-counter NSAIDs and St John's wort without advice.
