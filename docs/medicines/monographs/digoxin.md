# Digoxin

**Class / ATC:** Cardiac glycoside — C01AA05. **Narrow therapeutic index**; on high-alert lists in many settings.

## Dosing (adult, oral)
| Indication | Dose |
|------------|------|
| AF rate control — loading (if needed) | 0.75–1.5 mg total in divided doses over 24 h |
| Maintenance (AF or heart failure) | 62.5–250 micrograms once daily, by renal function, age and response |

Reduce dose in renal impairment and older adults; it is renally cleared. Write doses in **micrograms** to avoid 10-fold errors.

## Levels
Sample at least 6 h after a dose. Heart failure: aim for 0.5–0.9 ng/mL; higher levels add toxicity without benefit. Toxicity can occur within the "normal" range when potassium is low.

## Contraindications
Second- or third-degree heart block without a pacemaker; ventricular tachycardia or fibrillation; WPW with AF (accessory pathway conduction); hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy (unless AF with heart failure, specialist).

## Toxicity
Nausea, vomiting, anorexia, confusion, visual disturbance (yellow-green vision), bradycardia, heart block, ventricular arrhythmias. Worsened by **hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hypercalcemia, hypothyroidism, renal impairment**, older age.

**Antidote:** digoxin-specific antibody fragments (Fab) for life-threatening toxicity — see [antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md). Correct potassium.

## Interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| **Amiodarone** | Doubles digoxin levels — halve the digoxin dose and monitor |
| Verapamil, diltiazem, clarithromycin, quinidine, ciclosporin | Raised levels |
| Loop/thiazide diuretics | Hypokalemia increases toxicity |
| Beta-blockers | Additive bradycardia and AV block |

## Monitoring
Heart rate, potassium, magnesium and renal function regularly; level if toxicity suspected, renal function changes, or an interacting drug starts.

## Patient counselling
Report nausea, vomiting, visual changes or a slow or irregular pulse. Do not double a missed dose.
