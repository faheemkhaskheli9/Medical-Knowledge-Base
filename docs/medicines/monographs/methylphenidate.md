# Methylphenidate

**Class:** Central nervous system stimulant (US Schedule II controlled substance). Used for attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD).

## Dosing (FDA, immediate-release Ritalin tablets)
| Population | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Adult | Average 20–30 mg daily, given in divided doses 2 or 3 times daily, preferably 30–45 minutes before meals; maximum 60 mg daily |
| Paediatric, 6 years and older | Start 5 mg twice daily (before breakfast and lunch); increase by 5–10 mg weekly; daily doses above 60 mg are not recommended |
| Renal impairment, hepatic impairment, older adults | See label |

- **Modified-release products are not interchangeable** with immediate-release tablets or with each other (for example, Ritalin LA starts at 20 mg once daily, increased by 10 mg weekly). Check the product label. See [look-alike, sound-alike](../look-alike-sound-alike.md).
- Assess the risk of abuse, misuse and addiction before prescribing.

## Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to methylphenidate or other ingredients.
- **During treatment with a monoamine oxidase inhibitor (MAOI) or within 14 days of stopping one** — hypertensive crisis.
- Other product-specific contraindications: see label.

## Warnings / boxed warnings
- **Boxed warning — abuse, misuse and addiction:** misuse and abuse of CNS stimulants can cause overdose and death; risk is higher with higher doses or non-oral routes (snorting, injection).
- **Serious cardiac disease:** avoid in known structural cardiac abnormalities, cardiomyopathy, serious arrhythmias or coronary artery disease. Sudden death, stroke and myocardial infarction have been reported in adults; sudden death has occurred in patients with heart defects or serious heart disease.
- **Increased blood pressure and heart rate** (listed in Ritalin LA labeling; see label for each product).
- **Priapism:** prolonged, painful erections in males of all ages, sometimes needing surgery, often after a dose increase or during withdrawal — seek immediate care.
- **Peripheral vasculopathy, including Raynaud's phenomenon:** observe for digital changes; consider rheumatology referral.
- **Long-term growth suppression in children:** monitor height and weight; interrupt treatment if not growing or gaining weight as expected.

## Major interactions
| Drug | Effect / action |
|------|-----------------|
| MAOIs (including within 14 days of stopping) | Hypertensive crisis — contraindicated |
| Other interactions | See label and [major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md) |

## Adverse effects
- Common: see label.
- Serious: cardiovascular events, priapism, peripheral vasculopathy, growth suppression, abuse/dependence.

## Pregnancy / lactation
See label and [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
Before starting: cardiac history and risk of misuse. During treatment: blood pressure and heart rate; height and weight in children; fingers and toes for colour or temperature changes; signs of misuse or diversion. See [paediatrics](../../special-populations/pediatrics.md).

## Overdose / antidote
No specific antidote; supportive care — see label and [antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md).

## Patient counselling points
Take exactly as prescribed; never share — it is a controlled drug. Store securely. Tell your prescriber about any heart problems, and about any antidepressant called an MAOI. Seek urgent care for chest pain, fainting, or an erection that will not go away. Report fingers or toes that turn pale, blue or painful.
