# Montelukast

**Class / ATC:** Leukotriene receptor antagonist — R03DC03.

## Dosing (oral, once daily, in the evening for asthma)
| Age | Dose |
|-----|------|
| 15 years and older | 10 mg tablet |
| 6–14 years | 5 mg chewable tablet |
| 2–5 years | 4 mg chewable tablet or granules |
| Exercise-induced bronchoconstriction (15 years and older) | 10 mg at least 2 hours before exercise; no extra dose if already taking it daily, and no more than one dose in 24 hours (FDA) |

No dose adjustment for renal impairment or mild to moderate hepatic impairment. Younger children (from 6 months for some indications): 4 mg granules — use BNFc or the label.

## Indications and place in therapy
- Add-on asthma controller; usually less effective than inhaled corticosteroids (GINA). **Not a reliever** — does not treat an acute attack; use the prescribed reliever ([salbutamol](salbutamol.md)).
- Allergic rhinitis: FDA restricts use to patients who have not responded to, or cannot take, other treatments, because of the neuropsychiatric risk.

## Contraindications
Hypersensitivity to montelukast.

## Warnings
- **Boxed warning (FDA 2020) — serious neuropsychiatric events:** agitation, aggression, sleep disturbance, vivid dreams or nightmares, depression, suicidal thoughts and behaviour, including in people with no psychiatric history; may occur during treatment or after stopping. Discuss before prescribing; stop and seek advice if they occur.
- Do not abruptly substitute for inhaled or oral corticosteroids.
- **Eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (Churg-Strauss)** reported rarely, often when oral steroids are reduced — watch for vasculitic rash, worsening breathing, cardiac symptoms or neuropathy.
- Chewable tablets contain aspartame (phenylalanine) — caution in phenylketonuria.

## Interactions
Few clinically important. Strong enzyme inducers (phenobarbital, rifampicin) lower montelukast levels — monitor response.

## Adverse effects
- Common: headache, abdominal pain, upper respiratory infection, diarrhea.
- Serious: neuropsychiatric events (above), hypersensitivity reactions, EGPA, raised liver enzymes (rare).

## Pregnancy / lactation
Can be continued in pregnancy when needed for asthma control; available data have not shown a clear increase in major malformations. Limited breastfeeding data.

## Patient counselling
Take every evening even when well; it will not relieve an attack — keep your reliever inhaler with you. Tell your doctor straight away about mood or behaviour changes, sleep problems, nightmares, or thoughts of self-harm (see [crisis support](../../mental-health/crisis-support.md)). Seek urgent help if asthma symptoms are severe or the reliever is not helping ([red flags](../../guidelines/red-flags.md)).
