# Allopurinol

**Class / ATC:** Xanthine oxidase inhibitor (urate-lowering therapy) — M04AA01. Alternative: febuxostat.

## Dosing (adult, oral, once daily)
| Step | Dose |
|------|------|
| Start | 100 mg (≤ 50 mg in CKD stage 4 or worse) |
| Titrate | Increase by 100 mg every 2–5 weeks until serum urate < 6 mg/dL (360 µmol/L); < 5 mg/dL (300 µmol/L) for tophi |
| Max | 800 mg/day (US label); 900 mg/day (UK) |

"Start low, go slow" lowers the risk of hypersensitivity and flares; doses above eGFR-based limits are acceptable when titrated slowly with monitoring (ACR 2020).

## Warnings
- **Severe cutaneous reactions** (SJS/TEN, DRESS / allopurinol hypersensitivity syndrome), mostly in the first months. **Stop at the first sign of rash.**
- **HLA-B\*58:01** carries a much higher risk. ACR 2020 recommends testing before starting in people of Southeast Asian (e.g. Han Chinese, Korean, Thai) or African American ancestry; carriers should not receive allopurinol. See [pharmacogenomics](../../pharmacogenomics/gene-drug-pairs.md).
- Starting can trigger flares: give prophylaxis (colchicine, NSAID or low-dose prednisolone) for 3–6 months.
- Do not stop it during an acute flare if already taking it; you may start it during a flare with prophylaxis.

## Interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| **Azathioprine, mercaptopurine** | Severe myelosuppression — avoid, or reduce thiopurine to 25% of dose with specialist monitoring |
| Warfarin | Increased INR — monitor |
| Amoxicillin/ampicillin | More rash |
| Theophylline, ciclosporin | Raised levels |

## Adverse effects
Rash (most common), GI upset, raised liver enzymes, gout flares on initiation.

## Monitoring
Serum urate every 2–5 weeks while titrating, then every 6–12 months; renal and liver function.

## Patient counselling
Take daily for the long term, even without symptoms. Stop and seek urgent advice for any rash, fever, mouth ulcers or blistering. Drink plenty of fluids.
