# Enoxaparin (low molecular weight heparins)

**Class / ATC:** Low molecular weight heparin (LMWH), indirect factor Xa (and IIa) inhibitor — B01AB05. Others: dalteparin, tinzaparin. **High-alert medication** ([high-alert medications](../high-alert-medications.md)).

**Units:** 1 mg enoxaparin = 100 units anti-Xa activity (e.g. 40 mg = 4,000 units). LMWHs are **not** interchangeable unit-for-unit with each other or with unfractionated heparin.

## Dosing (adult, subcutaneous)
| Indication | Dose |
|------------|------|
| VTE prophylaxis (abdominal surgery, acutely ill medical patients) | 40 mg once daily |
| VTE prophylaxis after knee or hip replacement | 30 mg every 12 hours (US); or 40 mg once daily (hip replacement, US; also UK) |
| Treatment of DVT/PE | 1 mg/kg every 12 hours, or 1.5 mg/kg once daily (inpatient, US) — UK: 1.5 mg/kg once daily, or 1 mg/kg every 12 hours in patients with complicating risk factors |
| Unstable angina / NSTEMI | 1 mg/kg every 12 hours with aspirin |
| **Severe renal impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min)** | Prophylaxis: 30 mg once daily. Treatment: 1 mg/kg once daily (FDA) |

- Use actual body weight for treatment doses; consider anti-Xa monitoring in renal impairment, obesity, low body weight, pregnancy and children.
- STEMI regimens and pediatric doses are specialist — use the label or BNFc.

## Contraindications
- Active major bleeding.
- History of **heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT)** within the past 100 days, or circulating HIT antibodies.
- Hypersensitivity to enoxaparin, heparin or pork products; benzyl alcohol (multi-dose vials only).

## Warnings
- **Boxed warning — spinal/epidural hematoma** with neuraxial anesthesia or spinal puncture, which can cause permanent paralysis. Delay catheter placement or removal for at least 12 hours after a prophylactic dose and at least 24 hours after a treatment dose (1 mg/kg every 12 hours or 1.5 mg/kg once daily) (FDA, ASRA).
- **HIT:** monitor platelet count; stop if platelets fall substantially or new thrombosis occurs.
- **Hyperkalemia** (heparins suppress aldosterone) — risk higher with diabetes, CKD, and potassium-raising drugs.
- Mechanical heart valves (especially in pregnancy): valve thrombosis reported — specialist management.
- Bleeding risk higher with renal impairment, low body weight and older age.

## Interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Other anticoagulants, antiplatelets ([aspirin](aspirin.md), [clopidogrel](clopidogrel.md)), NSAIDs | Increased bleeding — avoid unless intended (e.g. ACS) |
| ACE inhibitors, ARBs, spironolactone, trimethoprim, potassium supplements | Hyperkalemia |

## Adverse effects
- Common: injection-site bruising, hematoma or pain; raised liver enzymes.
- Serious: major bleeding, HIT, spinal/epidural hematoma, hyperkalemia.

## Pregnancy / lactation
LMWH does not cross the placenta and is the usual anticoagulant in pregnancy. Use preservative-free (single-dose) presentations, as benzyl alcohol may cross the placenta. Compatible with breastfeeding.

## Monitoring
Baseline platelet count, hemoglobin, creatinine; platelets during treatment; potassium in patients at risk; anti-Xa level in the populations above.

## Overdose / reversal
**Protamine sulfate** gives partial reversal (neutralises about 60% of anti-Xa activity): 1 mg protamine per 1 mg enoxaparin given within the previous 8 hours; 0.5 mg protamine per 1 mg enoxaparin if more than 8 hours have passed or a second protamine dose is needed (FDA). See [antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md).

## Patient counselling
Inject into the abdomen (at least 5 cm from the navel), rotating sides; do not rub the site. Do not expel the air bubble from a prefilled syringe. Seek urgent help for black stools, blood in urine, vomiting blood, severe headache, or back pain with leg weakness or numbness after a spinal or epidural procedure.
