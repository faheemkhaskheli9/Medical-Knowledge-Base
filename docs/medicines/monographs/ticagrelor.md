# Ticagrelor

**Class / ATC:** Oral P2Y12 inhibitor (antiplatelet), reversible, not a prodrug — B01AC24. Compare [clopidogrel](clopidogrel.md); used with low-dose [aspirin](aspirin.md).

## Dosing (adult, FDA)
| Indication | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Acute coronary syndrome or history of MI | 180 mg loading dose, then 90 mg twice daily during the first year; after one year, 60 mg twice daily |
| Coronary artery disease without prior stroke or MI (high risk) | 60 mg twice daily |
| Acute ischemic stroke or high-risk TIA | 180 mg loading dose, then 90 mg twice daily for up to 30 days |

- **Aspirin dose:** with ticagrelor, use an aspirin maintenance dose of **75–100 mg daily**. Maintenance aspirin above 100 mg daily reduces the effectiveness of ticagrelor.
- No renal dose adjustment in the label; avoid in severe hepatic impairment (check label).
- Not affected by CYP2C19 genotype, unlike clopidogrel ([pharmacogenomics](../../pharmacogenomics/gene-drug-pairs.md)).

## Contraindications
- History of intracranial hemorrhage.
- Active pathological bleeding (e.g. peptic ulcer, intracranial hemorrhage).
- Hypersensitivity (including angioedema) to ticagrelor.

## Warnings / boxed warnings
- **Bleeding risk** (boxed warning): can cause significant, sometimes fatal, bleeding. Do not start in patients undergoing urgent CABG.
- **Premature discontinuation** increases the risk of MI, stroke, stent thrombosis and death. If it must be interrupted (e.g. for surgery with major bleeding risk, where possible stop 5 days before), restart as soon as hemostasis allows.
- **Dyspnea:** common and usually self-limiting; if intolerable, consider another antiplatelet.
- **Bradyarrhythmias:** ventricular pauses reported; caution in patients at risk of bradycardia without a pacemaker.

## Major interactions
| Drug | Effect / action |
|------|-----------------|
| Strong CYP3A inhibitors (e.g. ketoconazole, itraconazole, voriconazole, [clarithromycin](clarithromycin.md), ritonavir) | Large rise in ticagrelor exposure — bleeding, dyspnea — **avoid** |
| Strong CYP3A inducers (e.g. rifampicin, [phenytoin](phenytoin.md), [carbamazepine](carbamazepine.md), phenobarbital) | Large fall in exposure, loss of efficacy — **avoid** |
| Simvastatin or lovastatin above 40 mg/day | Higher statin levels, more statin adverse effects |
| Aspirin > 100 mg/day | Reduced ticagrelor effectiveness |
| Anticoagulants ([warfarin](warfarin.md), DOACs), NSAIDs, SSRIs | Additive bleeding risk |
| [Digoxin](digoxin.md) | Digoxin levels may rise — monitor |

See [major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md).

## Adverse effects
- Common: bleeding (bruising, nosebleeds), dyspnea.
- Serious: major or fatal bleeding, intracranial hemorrhage, bradyarrhythmia/ventricular pauses, hypersensitivity.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Limited human data — specialist advice. See [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
Bleeding, hemoglobin, adherence (twice-daily dosing), breathlessness, heart rate/rhythm symptoms, interacting drugs (especially antifungals, macrolides, HIV medicines, anticonvulsants).

## Overdose / antidote
No specific reversal agent in the FDA label; ticagrelor is not dialyzable. Platelet transfusion did not reverse its effect in healthy volunteers and is unlikely to help. Manage bleeding supportively with specialist (cardiology/haematology) advice ([antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md)).

## Patient counselling points
Take twice a day, every day, with low-dose aspirin only as prescribed — do not take extra aspirin or over-the-counter NSAIDs. Never stop without speaking to your cardiologist, especially after a stent. Mild breathlessness is common and often settles; report it. Tell dentists and surgeons you take it. Seek urgent help for black stools, vomiting blood, severe headache, fainting, or bleeding that will not stop.
