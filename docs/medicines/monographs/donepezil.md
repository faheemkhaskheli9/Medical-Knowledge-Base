# Donepezil

**Class / ATC:** Acetylcholinesterase inhibitor — N06DA02. Others: rivastigmine, galantamine; memantine is a different class.

## Dosing (adult, Alzheimer's dementia)
| Source | Dose |
|--------|------|
| FDA, mild to moderate | 5 mg once daily in the evening at bedtime; may increase to 10 mg once daily after 4–6 weeks at 5 mg |
| FDA, moderate to severe | 10 mg once daily; 23 mg once daily only after at least 3 months at 10 mg (do not split, crush or chew the 23 mg tablet) |
| UK SmPC | 5 mg once daily in the evening for at least one month, then may increase to 10 mg once daily; **maximum 10 mg daily** |

- Can be taken with or without food.
- No routine renal or hepatic dose adjustment in the labels; check the label for hepatic impairment.

## Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to donepezil or piperidine derivatives.

## Warnings / boxed warnings
- **Vagotonic effects:** bradycardia and heart block (with or without known conduction abnormalities); syncope reported. Caution with sick sinus syndrome or other conduction disorders and with [beta-blockers](bisoprolol.md) or [digoxin](digoxin.md).
- **QT prolongation and torsade de pointes** (post-marketing; UK SmPC) — caution with personal or family history of QTc prolongation, QT-prolonging drugs, or relevant cardiac disease.
- **Peptic ulcer / GI bleeding:** increased gastric acid — monitor for active or occult bleeding, especially with ulcer history or NSAIDs ([ibuprofen](ibuprofen.md)).
- **Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, weight loss** — observe after starting and after dose increases; weight loss is associated with the 23 mg dose.
- **Anesthesia:** likely to exaggerate succinylcholine-type muscle relaxation — tell the anesthetist.
- Bladder outflow obstruction; seizures (cholinomimetics may cause generalized convulsions; seizures can also be a feature of Alzheimer's disease); caution in asthma or COPD.
- Neuroleptic malignant syndrome reported very rarely, particularly with [antipsychotics](antipsychotics.md).

## Major interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Beta-blockers, [digoxin](digoxin.md), rate-limiting calcium-channel blockers | Additive bradycardia/heart block |
| Anticholinergic drugs (e.g. [amitriptyline](amitriptyline.md), oxybutynin, first-generation antihistamines) | Opposing effects — reduce benefit; review anticholinergic burden ([older adults](../../special-populations/older-adults.md)) |
| Succinylcholine-type muscle relaxants | Exaggerated neuromuscular blockade |
| Strong CYP3A4/CYP2D6 inhibitors or inducers | May raise or lower donepezil levels |
| QT-prolonging drugs (e.g. [citalopram](citalopram.md), [antipsychotics](antipsychotics.md)) | Additive QT risk |

See [major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md).

## Adverse effects
- Common: nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, insomnia, vivid dreams, muscle cramps, fatigue, decreased appetite.
- Serious: bradycardia, heart block, syncope (falls), GI bleeding, seizures, QT prolongation.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Not relevant for most patients; no adequate human data — see label. See [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
Pulse (bradycardia), falls and syncope, weight and appetite, GI bleeding symptoms, cognitive and functional response at review; ECG if cardiac risk factors.

## Overdose / antidote
Cholinergic crisis (severe nausea, vomiting, salivation, sweating, bradycardia, hypotension, respiratory depression, collapse, convulsions, increasing muscle weakness) — emergency care; tertiary anticholinergics such as atropine may be used as an antidote per label ([antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md)).

## Patient counselling points
Take once a day in the evening. If vivid dreams or sleep problems occur, discuss taking it in the morning with the prescriber. Report dizziness, fainting, slow pulse, black stools or vomiting blood. Tell any surgeon or anesthetist you take it. Do not stop or restart at a high dose without advice — if several doses are missed, the prescriber may restart at the lower dose.
