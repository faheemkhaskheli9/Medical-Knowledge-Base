# Tranexamic acid

**Class / ATC:** Antifibrinolytic (lysine analogue; blocks plasminogen binding to fibrin) — B02AA02. Used for heavy menstrual bleeding, local fibrinolysis (e.g. dental extraction, epistaxis, prostatectomy), hemophilia dental procedures, and trauma/obstetric hemorrhage protocols (IV).

## Dosing (adult)
| Indication / route | Dose |
|--------------------|------|
| Heavy menstrual bleeding — FDA (oral) | 1300 mg three times daily (3900 mg/day) for up to 5 days during menstruation; start only once the period has begun |
| Heavy menstrual bleeding — UK SmPC (oral) | 1 g (2 × 500 mg tablets) three times daily for up to 4 days; do not exceed 4 g daily; start only once bleeding has started |
| Local fibrinolysis — UK SmPC (oral) | 15–25 mg/kg two to three times daily |
| Hemophilia, tooth extraction — FDA (IV) | 10 mg/kg actual body weight 3–4 times daily, for 2–8 days |

**Renal impairment (FDA oral, heavy menstrual bleeding — by serum creatinine):**

| Serum creatinine | Dose |
|------------------|------|
| > 1.4 to 2.8 mg/dL | 1300 mg twice daily (2600 mg/day) |
| > 2.8 to 5.7 mg/dL | 1300 mg once daily |
| > 5.7 mg/dL | 650 mg once daily |

UK IV renal reduction (Cyklokapron SmPC): serum creatinine 120–249 µmol/L: 10 mg/kg every 12 hours; 250–500 µmol/L: 10 mg/kg every 24 hours; > 500 µmol/L: 5 mg/kg every 24 hours. Do not apply IV tables to tablets; check the oral SmPC table.

## Contraindications
- Active thromboembolic disease, or history or intrinsic risk of thrombosis or thromboembolism, including retinal vein or artery occlusion (FDA oral).
- **Concomitant combined hormonal contraception** (FDA oral) — increased thrombosis risk.
- **Injection: neuraxial (intrathecal, epidural) administration** — contraindicated.
- Hypersensitivity to tranexamic acid.
- Check the SmPC for further contraindications (e.g. severe renal impairment, history of convulsions — product-specific).

## Warnings / boxed warnings
- **Injection is for intravenous use only (boxed warning):** serious, including fatal, reactions with seizures and cardiac arrhythmias after inadvertent intrathecal injection — usually when mistaken for a local anesthetic. Store separately from local anesthetics and verify the label before neuraxial procedures.
- **Thromboembolic risk** — higher with hormonal contraception, factor IX complex or anti-inhibitor coagulant concentrates, and all-trans retinoic acid.
- **Seizures**, mainly with high IV doses during cardiovascular surgery.
- Visual disturbances, including retinal vein/artery occlusion — stop and refer for ophthalmology review.
- Hematuria from the upper urinary tract: risk of ureteric obstruction.
- Heavy menstrual bleeding that does not improve needs gynecological assessment.

## Major interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Combined hormonal contraceptives | Thrombosis — contraindicated with oral TXA (FDA) |
| Factor IX complex, anti-inhibitor coagulant complex | Thrombosis risk |
| All-trans retinoic acid (tretinoin, for APL) | Procoagulant effect may worsen |

See [major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md).

## Adverse effects
- Common: headache, back or abdominal pain, nausea, diarrhea, nasal/sinus symptoms, muscle pain.
- Serious: thromboembolism (DVT, PE, stroke, retinal occlusion), seizures, hypersensitivity, visual disturbance; catastrophic neurotoxicity if given intrathecally.

## Pregnancy / lactation
The oral product for heavy menstrual bleeding is not for use in pregnancy (not indicated). IV use in obstetric hemorrhage follows specialist protocols. See [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
Bleeding response, renal function (dose), signs of thrombosis, vision; confirm route before any IV administration in theatre.

## Overdose / antidote
No specific antidote; supportive care. Watch for thrombosis, seizures and hypotension ([antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md)).

## Patient counselling points
For heavy periods, start tablets only once your period has started, and do not take them for longer than the stated number of days. Do not use with the combined pill, patch or ring unless your doctor says so. Seek urgent help for leg pain or swelling, chest pain or breathlessness, sudden weakness, or changes in vision. See your doctor if heavy bleeding does not improve over several cycles.
