# Citalopram

**Class / ATC:** Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) — N06AB04. Escitalopram is its S-enantiomer and shares the QT warning. Compare [sertraline](sertraline.md).

## Dosing (adult)
| Population | Dose | Max |
|------------|------|-----|
| Depression, adult (FDA and UK) | 20 mg once daily | **40 mg once daily** — higher doses not recommended (QT prolongation) |
| Older adults | UK: 10–20 mg once daily | FDA: 20 mg/day if > 60 years; UK: 20 mg/day if > 65 years |
| Hepatic impairment | — | 20 mg/day (FDA) |
| CYP2C19 poor metabolizers, or taking cimetidine or another CYP2C19 inhibitor | — | 20 mg/day (FDA) |

- Taper gradually when stopping.
- Not approved for children (FDA).

## Contraindications
- MAOIs (including linezolid or IV methylene blue) — or within 14 days of stopping an MAOI.
- Pimozide.
- UK SmPC also: known QT prolongation or congenital long QT syndrome; use with other medicines known to prolong the QT interval.

## Warnings / boxed warnings
- **Suicidal thoughts and behaviors** in children, adolescents and young adults (boxed warning) — monitor closely, especially early and after dose changes.
- **Dose-dependent QT prolongation and torsade de pointes.** Avoid in congenital long QT, bradycardia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, recent MI or uncompensated heart failure; check electrolytes in at-risk patients; ECG if risk factors.
- **Serotonin syndrome** with other serotonergic drugs (triptans — [sumatriptan](sumatriptan.md), [tramadol and other opioids](opioids.md), [venlafaxine](venlafaxine.md), [lithium](lithium.md), St John's wort).
- **Bleeding risk** with NSAIDs, aspirin, anticoagulants.
- **Hyponatremia/SIADH**, especially in older adults and with diuretics.
- Activation of mania/hypomania; angle-closure glaucoma; discontinuation symptoms.

## Major interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| MAOIs, linezolid, methylene blue | Serotonin syndrome — contraindicated |
| Other QT-prolonging drugs (e.g. [hydroxychloroquine](hydroxychloroquine.md), [ondansetron](ondansetron.md), [antipsychotics](antipsychotics.md), macrolides, [fluconazole](fluconazole.md)) | Additive QT prolongation — UK: contraindicated |
| Cimetidine, omeprazole and other CYP2C19 inhibitors | Raise citalopram levels — FDA max 20 mg/day with cimetidine or CYP2C19 inhibitors |
| NSAIDs, aspirin, [warfarin](warfarin.md), DOACs | Bleeding |
| Triptans, tramadol, other serotonergic drugs | Serotonin syndrome |

See [major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md) and [pharmacogenomics](../../pharmacogenomics/gene-drug-pairs.md).

## Adverse effects
- Common: nausea, dry mouth, sweating, somnolence or insomnia, sexual dysfunction.
- Serious: QT prolongation/torsade de pointes, serotonin syndrome, hyponatremia, bleeding, suicidality in young people, mania.

## Pregnancy / lactation
SSRIs may be continued in pregnancy when benefit outweighs risk (small risk of persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn and neonatal adaptation symptoms). See [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
Mood and suicidality (especially early in treatment and after dose changes), sodium in older adults, ECG and potassium/magnesium if cardiac risk factors or interacting drugs.

## Overdose / antidote
Overdose can cause seizures, QT prolongation and arrhythmia — treat as an emergency; ECG monitoring and supportive care ([antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md)).

## Patient counselling points
It can take a few weeks to feel better. Do not stop suddenly — talk to your prescriber about tapering. Tell your pharmacist about all other medicines, including St John's wort, migraine tablets and heart rhythm medicines. Seek urgent help for fainting, a racing or irregular heartbeat, thoughts of self-harm, or confusion with high temperature, shaking and agitation.
