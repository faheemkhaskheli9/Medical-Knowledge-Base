# Oxybutynin

**Class / ATC:** Antimuscarinic (anticholinergic) for overactive bladder — G04BD04. Others: solifenacin, tolterodine, trospium, fesoterodine; mirabegron is a beta-3 agonist alternative.

## Dosing
| Product / population | Dose |
|----------------------|------|
| Immediate-release, adult (FDA) | 5 mg two or three times daily; maximum 5 mg four times daily |
| Immediate-release, frail older adults (FDA) | Start 2.5 mg two or three times daily |
| Immediate-release, children over 5 years (FDA) | 5 mg twice daily; maximum 5 mg three times daily |
| Extended-release, adult | 5 mg or 10 mg once daily; adjust in 5 mg steps. **Maximum differs by product label** (Ditropan XL 2018 label: 20 mg/day; some generic ER labels: 30 mg/day) — check the product |

- Swallow ER tablets whole.
- Transdermal and gel forms exist with different doses and less dry mouth.

## Contraindications
- Urinary retention.
- Gastric retention and other severe decreased GI motility.
- Uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma, and patients at risk of these conditions.
- Hypersensitivity.

## Warnings / boxed warnings
- **Anticholinergic CNS effects:** confusion, hallucinations, somnolence, agitation — especially in older adults. Caution in dementia, and when combined with cholinesterase inhibitors such as [donepezil](donepezil.md) (opposing effects).
- **Dementia risk (observational):** a 2024 nested case-control study (BMJ Medicine) associated long-term oxybutynin use with higher dementia risk. Association, not proven causation — consider total anticholinergic burden in older adults.
- **NICE NG123:** do not offer immediate-release oxybutynin to frail older women (check the current wording).
- **Urinary retention** in bladder outflow obstruction; **GI obstruction/reduced motility**; heat prostration (reduced sweating); angioedema.
- Myasthenia gravis, autonomic neuropathy, reflux oesophagitis: caution.

## Major interactions
| Drug | Effect / action |
|------|-----------------|
| Other anticholinergics (e.g. [amitriptyline](amitriptyline.md), antihistamines, some [antipsychotics](antipsychotics.md)) | Additive anticholinergic burden — confusion, constipation, retention |
| Cholinesterase inhibitors ([donepezil](donepezil.md)) | Opposing effects; may worsen cognition |
| Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (ketoconazole, itraconazole, [clarithromycin](clarithromycin.md)) | Higher oxybutynin levels |
| Bisphosphonates ([alendronate](alendronate.md)), potassium chloride tablets | Reduced GI motility may increase oesophageal irritation |

See [major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md).

## Adverse effects
- Common: dry mouth (very common), constipation, blurred vision, dizziness, drowsiness.
- Serious: confusion/delirium, urinary retention, angle-closure glaucoma, heat stroke, angioedema.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Limited data — specialist advice. See [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
Response and side effects after 4 weeks; post-void residual if retention suspected; cognition in older adults; review need regularly. See [older adults](../../special-populations/older-adults.md).

## Overdose / antidote
Anticholinergic syndrome: delirium, tachycardia, hyperthermia, dry flushed skin, urinary retention. Supportive care; physostigmine only with specialist/poison centre advice ([antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md)).

## Patient counselling points
Dry mouth is common — sugar-free gum or sips of water help. Avoid overheating (you may sweat less). It may cause drowsiness or blurred vision — take care driving. Tell your doctor if you cannot pass urine, become confused, or have eye pain with blurred vision (urgent).
