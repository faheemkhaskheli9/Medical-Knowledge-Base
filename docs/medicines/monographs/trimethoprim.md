# Trimethoprim and co-trimoxazole

**Class / ATC:** Dihydrofolate reductase inhibitor — trimethoprim J01EA01; co-trimoxazole (sulfamethoxazole + trimethoprim, 5:1) J01EE01. WHO AWaRe **Access** group.

## Dosing (adult, oral)
| Indication | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Lower UTI (cystitis), non-pregnant women | Trimethoprim 200 mg twice daily for 3 days, if low resistance risk (NICE NG109) |
| Lower UTI, men | Trimethoprim 200 mg twice daily for 7 days (NICE NG109) |
| Co-trimoxazole (where indicated, e.g. *Pneumocystis* treatment/prophylaxis, specific infections) | Usual adult dose 960 mg (800 mg/160 mg) twice daily; *Pneumocystis* treatment uses higher weight-based doses — specialist |

- NICE: use trimethoprim for cystitis only if resistance risk is low (no use in the past 3 months, not older and in residential care). Nitrofurantoin is the usual first choice when eGFR ≥ 45 ([nitrofurantoin](nitrofurantoin.md)). Follow local antibiograms ([stewardship](../../infections/antimicrobial-stewardship.md)).
- UK: co-trimoxazole is restricted to specific indications because of serious adverse effects.
- Renal impairment: reduce dose when eGFR < 30 (BNF); check the current BNF/label.

## Contraindications
Blood dyscrasias, including megaloblastic anemia from folate deficiency. Co-trimoxazole: also sulfonamide antibiotic allergy, severe hepatic impairment, infants under 6 weeks (US label: under 2 months; kernicterus risk).

## Warnings
- **Hyperkalemia** (amiloride-like effect on the distal tubule) — older adults, CKD, and with ACE inhibitors, ARBs, spironolactone. Serious when combined with spironolactone in older adults.
- Raises serum creatinine by blocking tubular secretion without a true fall in GFR — interpret creatinine with care.
- Co-trimoxazole: Stevens–Johnson syndrome/TEN, bone marrow suppression, hepatitis, hyponatremia; higher adverse-reaction rate in HIV.

## Interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| **Methotrexate** | Bone marrow suppression, deaths — avoid ([methotrexate](methotrexate.md)) |
| ACE inhibitors, ARBs, spironolactone, potassium supplements | Hyperkalemia |
| Warfarin | Co-trimoxazole markedly raises INR — avoid or monitor INR closely ([warfarin](warfarin.md)) |
| Phenytoin | Raised phenytoin levels |
| Other folate antagonists (e.g. pyrimethamine) | Additive bone marrow toxicity |

## Adverse effects
- Common: nausea, rash, itch.
- Serious: hyperkalemia, severe cutaneous reactions, blood dyscrasias, aseptic meningitis (rare).

## Pregnancy / lactation
Avoid in pregnancy, especially the first trimester (folate antagonist, neural tube defect risk); NICE NG109 recommends other options for UTI in pregnancy. Breastfeeding: short courses usually acceptable in healthy term infants; avoid co-trimoxazole in jaundiced, premature or G6PD-deficient infants.

## Monitoring
Potassium and creatinine in those at risk of hyperkalemia; FBC with long-term use.

## Patient counselling
Complete the course. Seek advice for rash, sore throat, fever, mouth ulcers or unusual bruising. Tell the prescriber about methotrexate, warfarin or potassium-raising medicines.
