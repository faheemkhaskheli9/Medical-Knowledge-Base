# Diazepam (benzodiazepines)

**Class / ATC:** Long-acting benzodiazepine; GABA-A positive modulator — N05BA01. Others: lorazepam, alprazolam, clonazepam, temazepam, chlordiazepoxide. Long half-life with an active metabolite (desmethyldiazepam), so it accumulates, especially in older adults and liver disease. Controlled drug in most countries.

## Dosing (oral, adult)
| Indication | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Anxiety (FDA) | 2–10 mg 2–4 times daily, depending on severity |
| Anxiety (UK, BNF) | 2 mg three times daily, increased if needed to 15–30 mg daily in divided doses; **short-term use only (2–4 weeks)** |
| Acute alcohol withdrawal (FDA) | 10 mg 3–4 times during the first 24 hours, then 5 mg 3–4 times daily as needed. Follow a local symptom-triggered protocol where available |
| Muscle spasm (adjunct, FDA) | 2–10 mg 3–4 times daily |
| Older adults or debilitated | Lowest dose (e.g. FDA: 2–2.5 mg once or twice daily; BNF: half the adult dose), increase gradually |

- Status epilepticus and prolonged seizures: rectal, IV or other routes per emergency protocols (buccal or intranasal midazolam are common alternatives) — follow local guidance.
- Hepatic impairment: reduce dose; avoid in severe impairment. Children: specialist and weight-based (BNFc).

## Contraindications
- Severe respiratory insufficiency, sleep apnea syndrome.
- Myasthenia gravis.
- Severe hepatic impairment.
- Acute narrow-angle glaucoma (FDA).
- Hypersensitivity. FDA oral label: not for children under 6 months.

## Warnings
- **Boxed warning — concomitant opioids:** profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma and death. Co-prescribe only if no alternative, at the lowest doses and shortest duration ([opioids](opioids.md)).
- **Boxed warning — abuse, misuse and addiction; dependence and withdrawal.** After regular use, **do not stop abruptly** — withdrawal can cause seizures and can be life-threatening. Taper gradually.
- Not recommended as sole treatment of depression or anxiety with depression (suicide risk). See [crisis support](../../mental-health/crisis-support.md).
- Older adults: falls, fractures, confusion — avoid where possible (Beers) ([older adults](../../special-populations/older-adults.md)).
- Paradoxical agitation or aggression; anterograde amnesia; impaired driving the next day.

## Interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Opioids | Boxed warning — respiratory depression, death |
| Alcohol, [gabapentinoids](gabapentinoids.md), sedating antihistamines, antipsychotics, [amitriptyline](amitriptyline.md), [mirtazapine](mirtazapine.md) | Additive CNS and respiratory depression |
| CYP3A4/CYP2C19 inhibitors ([fluconazole](fluconazole.md), ketoconazole, [omeprazole](omeprazole.md), cimetidine) | Raise diazepam levels — prolonged sedation |
| CYP inducers (rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin) | Lower diazepam levels |

## Adverse effects
- Common: drowsiness, fatigue, ataxia, muscle weakness, impaired concentration.
- Serious: respiratory depression (especially with opioids or alcohol), dependence, withdrawal seizures, falls.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Use near delivery can cause neonatal sedation, respiratory depression, hypotonia ("floppy infant") and neonatal withdrawal (FDA 2020) — monitor the newborn. Passes into breast milk and can accumulate in the infant; avoid regular use while breastfeeding.

## Monitoring
Need for continued use at every review; signs of dependence; respiratory status when combined with other depressants.

## Overdose / antidote
Drowsiness, ataxia, confusion; coma and respiratory depression mainly with co-ingestants (opioids, alcohol). Supportive care with airway protection. **Flumazenil** is not routine — it can precipitate seizures in benzodiazepine dependence or with pro-convulsant co-ingestants such as TCAs ([antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md)).

## Patient counselling
Use only as prescribed, usually for a short time. Do not drink alcohol or take opioids or other sleep-inducing medicines unless the prescriber agrees. Do not drive or operate machinery if drowsy. After regular use, do not stop suddenly — the prescriber will plan a gradual reduction. Store securely and never share.
