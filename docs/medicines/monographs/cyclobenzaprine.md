# Cyclobenzaprine

**Class:** Centrally acting skeletal muscle relaxant, structurally related to tricyclic antidepressants (see [amitriptyline](amitriptyline.md)). For spasticity from neurological disease, see [baclofen](baclofen.md) and [tizanidine](tizanidine.md) instead — cyclobenzaprine is not effective for spasticity from cerebral or spinal cord disease.

## Dosing (adult, FDA immediate-release tablets)
| Population | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Adult (acute painful musculoskeletal conditions, adjunct to rest and physical therapy) | **5 mg PO three times daily**; may increase to 10 mg three times daily based on response |
| Duration | **Short term only — use for longer than 2 to 3 weeks is not recommended** |
| Older adults | Start 5 mg and titrate slowly (higher plasma levels; see label) |
| Mild hepatic impairment | Start 5 mg and titrate slowly |
| Moderate or severe hepatic impairment | **Not recommended** |
| Paediatric (under 15 years), renal impairment | See label |

- Extended-release capsules (e.g. Amrix) are dosed once daily and are not interchangeable mg-for-mg with tablets — see that label.
- Listed as potentially inappropriate in older adults (anticholinergic, sedation) — see [older adults](../../special-populations/older-adults.md).

## Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to cyclobenzaprine.
- **MAOIs** at the same time or within 14 days after stopping them (hyperpyretic crises, severe convulsions and deaths reported with tricyclic-like drugs).
- **Acute recovery phase of myocardial infarction**; arrhythmias, heart block or conduction disturbances; congestive heart failure.
- **Hyperthyroidism.**

## Warnings / boxed warnings
- **Serotonin syndrome** with SSRIs, SNRIs, tricyclics, tramadol, bupropion, meperidine (pethidine), verapamil or MAOIs — stop if suspected.
- Tricyclic-like effects: arrhythmias, sinus tachycardia, QT and conduction prolongation; stroke and MI have been reported with tricyclics.
- **Anticholinergic effects:** use caution with urinary retention, angle-closure glaucoma, raised intraocular pressure.
- **CNS depression:** additive with alcohol, barbiturates and other CNS depressants; impairs driving.

## Major interactions
| Drug | Effect / action |
|------|-----------------|
| MAOIs | Contraindicated (see above) |
| Serotonergic drugs ([sertraline](sertraline.md), [fluoxetine](fluoxetine.md), [venlafaxine](venlafaxine.md), tramadol, [bupropion](bupropion.md)) | Serotonin syndrome risk |
| Alcohol, benzodiazepines ([diazepam](diazepam.md)), [opioids](opioids.md), other sedatives | Additive CNS depression |
| Guanethidine and similar | Antihypertensive effect may be blocked |
| Tramadol | Seizure risk may be increased |

See [major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md).

## Adverse effects
- Common: drowsiness, dry mouth, dizziness, fatigue — see label.
- Serious: serotonin syndrome, arrhythmias, seizures, anticholinergic toxicity — see label.

## Pregnancy / lactation
See label and [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
Sedation and falls risk (especially older adults); stop after 2–3 weeks; cardiac history before prescribing; serotonergic co-medication.

## Overdose / antidote
Overdose can resemble tricyclic toxicity (arrhythmias, seizures, coma, hypotension) and can be fatal — get emergency care; ECG monitoring; supportive care — see label and [antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md).

## Patient counselling points
For short-term use only (no more than 2–3 weeks). It can make you drowsy — do not drive or use machines until you know how it affects you, and avoid alcohol. Tell your prescriber about antidepressants, tramadol, migraine or sleep medicines. Seek urgent care for a fast or irregular heartbeat, fainting, confusion, high temperature with shaking or muscle twitching, or being unable to pass urine.
