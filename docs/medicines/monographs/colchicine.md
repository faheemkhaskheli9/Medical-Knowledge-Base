# Colchicine

**Class / ATC:** Anti-inflammatory (microtubule inhibitor) — M04AC01. **Narrow therapeutic index.**

## Dosing (adult, oral)
| Indication | US (FDA) | UK (BNF) |
|------------|----------|----------|
| Acute gout flare (start as early as possible) | 1.2 mg at first sign, then 0.6 mg 1 h later (max 1.8 mg over 1 h) | 500 micrograms 2–4 times daily until symptoms relieved; max 6 mg per course; do not repeat a course within 3 days |
| Flare prophylaxis when starting urate-lowering therapy | 0.6 mg once or twice daily (max 1.2 mg/day) | 500 micrograms twice daily |
| Cardiovascular risk reduction (stable atherosclerotic disease) | 0.5 mg once daily (Lodoco) | — |

- Low-dose flare regimens are as effective as older high-dose regimens with far less diarrhea (ACR 2020).
- **Renal and hepatic impairment:** reduce dose or frequency; avoid repeat flare courses in dialysis; BNF: avoid if eGFR < 10. Older adults: lower doses.
- For starting allopurinol, see [allopurinol](allopurinol.md).

## Contraindications
**Renal or hepatic impairment combined with a P-gp or strong CYP3A4 inhibitor** (FDA). Blood dyscrasias (BNF: caution/avoid). Lodoco: pre-existing blood dyscrasias, eGFR < 15 or severe hepatic impairment (label).

## Warnings
- **Toxicity:** early GI symptoms (diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain) are the warning sign — stop and seek advice. Severe toxicity: bone marrow suppression, rhabdomyolysis, multi-organ failure, death; no specific antidote.
- **Myopathy/rhabdomyolysis and neuropathy**, especially with renal impairment, statins or fibrates.
- Keep away from children — small overdoses can be fatal.

## Interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| **Clarithromycin**, erythromycin, ketoconazole, itraconazole, ritonavir and other strong CYP3A4 inhibitors; **ciclosporin**, verapamil, diltiazem (P-gp/CYP3A4 inhibitors) | Raised colchicine levels — fatal toxicity reported. Avoid or reduce colchicine dose per label; contraindicated with renal/hepatic impairment ([major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md)) |
| Grapefruit juice | Raised levels — avoid large amounts |
| Statins, fibrates, digoxin | Myopathy/rhabdomyolysis |

## Adverse effects
- Common: diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain.
- Serious: myelosuppression, myopathy, neuropathy, rhabdomyolysis.

## Pregnancy / lactation
Specialist decision; continued in familial Mediterranean fever where benefit is established. Small amounts in breast milk.

## Overdose
Delayed multi-organ toxicity (GI phase, then bone marrow failure, shock); treat as life-threatening even if initially well — contact a poison center. See [antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md).

## Patient counselling
Do not take more than prescribed, even if pain continues. Stop and seek advice if diarrhea or vomiting develops. Tell prescribers and pharmacists — many antibiotics, antifungals and heart medicines interact. Report muscle pain or weakness, numbness, unusual bleeding or infections.
