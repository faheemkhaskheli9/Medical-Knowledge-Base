# Ferrous sulfate (oral iron)

**Class / ATC:** Oral iron (ferrous salt) — B03AA07. Other salts: ferrous fumarate, ferrous gluconate. **Always check elemental iron content**, not just the salt weight: ferrous sulfate 200 mg tablet ≈ 65 mg elemental iron; ferrous fumarate 210 mg ≈ 65–70 mg (UK products). Intravenous iron is a specialist option when oral iron fails or is not tolerated.

## Indications
Treatment and prevention of iron deficiency and iron-deficiency anemia. **Always look for the cause** — in adults, especially men and postmenopausal women, unexplained iron deficiency needs investigation for gastrointestinal blood loss or malignancy ([red flags](../../guidelines/red-flags.md)).

## Dosing (oral, adult, UK)
| Use | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Treatment of iron-deficiency anemia | Ferrous sulfate 200 mg (65 mg elemental iron) **once daily**; if not tolerated, the same dose on **alternate days** or try another salt (NICE CKS-based UK guidance) |
| Prophylaxis (BNF) | Ferrous sulfate 200 mg once or twice daily (60–120 mg elemental iron per day) |
| Children | Weight-based — BNFc |
| Pregnancy | Per national antenatal guidance |

- Once-daily or alternate-day dosing is now preferred to older 2–3 times daily regimens: better tolerated, with similar absorption per dose.
- Check hemoglobin after about 2–4 weeks (expect a rise); continue for about **3 months after hemoglobin normalizes** to replenish stores (NICE CKS).
- Best absorbed on an empty stomach; take with food if gastrointestinal effects occur. Vitamin C may aid absorption; tea, coffee, milk and calcium reduce it.

## Contraindications
- Iron overload (hemochromatosis, hemosiderosis) and anemias not due to iron deficiency (e.g. thalassemia without deficiency) unless iron deficient.
- Repeated blood transfusions (risk of iron overload).
- Hypersensitivity.

## Warnings
- **Accidental overdose in children is dangerous and can be fatal** — store out of sight and reach of children (US labeling warning).
- Can mask or delay diagnosis of a bleeding source if given without investigation.
- Caution in inflammatory bowel disease, strictures or diverticular disease.
- Do not give with IV iron.

## Major interactions
Iron reduces absorption of several drugs; antacids and calcium reduce iron absorption.

| Drug | Action |
|------|--------|
| [Levothyroxine](levothyroxine.md) | Separate by at least 4 hours |
| Tetracyclines ([doxycycline](doxycycline.md)) | Separate by 2–3 hours |
| Fluoroquinolones ([ciprofloxacin](ciprofloxacin.md)) | Take ciprofloxacin 2 hours before or 6 hours after iron |
| Bisphosphonates ([alendronate](alendronate.md)) | Take alendronate first on an empty stomach; iron later in the day |
| Antacids, calcium supplements, PPIs ([omeprazole](omeprazole.md)) | Reduce iron absorption — separate doses |
| Levodopa, mycophenolate, penicillamine, zinc | Reduced absorption — separate per product information |

## Adverse effects
- Common: nausea, epigastric pain, constipation or diarrhea, **dark or black stools** (harmless, but can be confused with melena).
- Serious: rare hypersensitivity; mouth ulceration or esophageal injury if a tablet lodges (take upright with water).

## Pregnancy / lactation
Safe and commonly used in pregnancy when iron deficient. Compatible with breastfeeding. See [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
Hemoglobin at 2–4 weeks; full blood count and ferritin after the course; investigation of the cause.

## Overdose / antidote
An acute ingestion of 75 mg/kg elemental iron is considered extremely dangerous in young children (UK SmPC); lower amounts can cause toxicity. Early vomiting, diarrhea and abdominal pain may be followed by apparent recovery, then shock, metabolic acidosis, liver failure and later gut strictures. **Emergency — contact a poison center.** Antidote for severe poisoning: **deferoxamine (desferrioxamine)**; activated charcoal does not bind iron ([antidotes](../../toxicology/antidotes.md)).

## Patient counselling points
Take one tablet once a day (or on alternate days if your doctor advises), ideally on an empty stomach; take with food if it upsets your stomach. Do not take at the same time as tea, coffee, milk, antacids or calcium. Stools will turn dark — but see a doctor urgently for black, sticky, tarry stools with dizziness, or vomiting blood. Keep iron tablets locked away from children. Keep taking it for about 3 months after your blood count is normal.
