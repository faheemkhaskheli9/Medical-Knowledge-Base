# Salbutamol (albuterol)

**Class / ATC:** Short-acting beta-2 agonist (SABA) — R03AC02. Named albuterol in the US.

## Dosing (metered-dose inhaler, 100 mcg/puff; US labels 90 mcg/actuation)
| Use | Dose |
|-----|------|
| Relief of symptoms | 1–2 puffs as needed |
| Exercise-induced bronchoconstriction | 2 puffs 5–20 min before exercise |
| Acute attack (awaiting help) | Up to 10 puffs via spacer, one at a time; repeat per action plan — call emergency services if no improvement |

Use a **spacer** with MDIs, especially in children and during attacks.

## Key guidance (GINA)
- **SABA-only treatment is no longer recommended** for adults and adolescents — every person with asthma should receive inhaled corticosteroid (ICS)-containing treatment.
- Preferred reliever for adults/adolescents: as-needed low-dose **ICS-formoterol** (GINA Track 1).
- Needing a reliever on > 2 days per week or night waking = poorly controlled asthma — review treatment.
- High SABA use (≥ 3 canisters/year) is linked to severe exacerbations and asthma death.

## Adverse effects
Tremor, palpitations, tachycardia, headache, hypokalemia (high doses).

## Emergency signs
Unable to speak in sentences, reliever not helping, blue lips, drowsiness, exhaustion → emergency services.
