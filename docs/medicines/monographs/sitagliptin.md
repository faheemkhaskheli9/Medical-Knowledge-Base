# Sitagliptin

**Class / ATC:** Dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) inhibitor ("gliptin") — A10BH01. Other gliptins (linagliptin, alogliptin, saxagliptin) differ in renal dosing — check each label. See [type 2 diabetes](../../conditions/type-2-diabetes.md), [metformin](metformin.md), [SGLT2 inhibitors](sglt2-inhibitors.md).

**Mechanism:** Prolongs the action of incretin hormones (GLP-1, GIP), increasing glucose-dependent insulin release and lowering glucagon. Low hypoglycemia risk on its own.

## Dosing (adult, FDA)
| Population | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Adult, eGFR ≥ 45 mL/min/1.73 m² | 100 mg once daily, with or without food |
| eGFR 30 to < 45 mL/min/1.73 m² | 50 mg once daily |
| eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m², including ESRD on hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis | 25 mg once daily, without regard to timing of dialysis |

- Assess renal function before starting and periodically.
- Not for type 1 diabetes or diabetic ketoacidosis.
- Combination products (e.g. with metformin) follow the metformin renal limits as well.

## Contraindications
- History of serious hypersensitivity to sitagliptin (e.g. anaphylaxis, angioedema, exfoliative skin conditions such as Stevens-Johnson syndrome).

## Warnings / boxed warnings
- **Pancreatitis:** acute pancreatitis, including fatal hemorrhagic or necrotizing cases, reported. Stop promptly if suspected; not studied in patients with prior pancreatitis.
- **Heart failure:** observed with two other DPP-4 inhibitors — weigh risks in patients with heart-failure risk factors and monitor.
- **Acute kidney injury:** reported postmarketing, sometimes requiring dialysis.
- **Hypoglycemia** when combined with insulin or a sulfonylurea ([gliclazide](gliclazide.md), [insulin](insulin.md)) — a lower dose of the insulin or sulfonylurea may be needed.
- **Bullous pemphigoid:** stop if suspected; ask patients to report blisters or erosions.
- Severe and disabling joint pain (arthralgia) reported with DPP-4 inhibitors.
- No boxed warning.

## Major interactions
| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Insulin, sulfonylureas | Hypoglycemia — consider dose reduction of these |
| [Digoxin](digoxin.md) | Small rise in digoxin levels — monitor as usual |

Few pharmacokinetic interactions. See [major interactions](../../interactions/major-interactions.md).

## Adverse effects
- Common: upper respiratory tract infection, nasopharyngitis, headache.
- Serious: pancreatitis, hypersensitivity (anaphylaxis, angioedema, SJS), bullous pemphigoid, acute kidney injury, heart failure (class signal).

## Pregnancy / lactation
Insulin is the usual choice for diabetes in pregnancy; data on sitagliptin are limited. See [pregnancy and lactation](../../special-populations/pregnancy-lactation.md).

## Monitoring
HbA1c, renal function (dose band), symptoms of pancreatitis, skin blistering, heart-failure symptoms, hypoglycemia if on insulin/sulfonylurea.

## Overdose / antidote
No specific antidote; supportive care. Modestly removed by hemodialysis.

## Patient counselling points
Take once daily at any time, with or without food. On its own it rarely causes low blood sugar, but it can with insulin or sulfonylureas — learn the symptoms. Stop and seek urgent care for severe persistent stomach pain (possibly spreading to the back) with or without vomiting. Report blisters, severe joint pain, swelling of the face or throat, or breathlessness and ankle swelling.
