# Non-sugar sweeteners: sucralose and acesulfame K (evidence update, 2026)

**Evidence checked:** 2026-11-18. **Scope:** general adult dietary consumption, particularly sugar-free carbonated beverages. This is a research summary, not an individualized medical recommendation.

## Bottom line

- **Safety assessment is not the same as demonstrated health benefit.** The US FDA authorizes both sucralose (E955) and acesulfame potassium (Ace-K, E950), subject to approved conditions and acceptable daily intake (ADI) values. EFSA's 2025 Ace-K and 2026 sucralose re-evaluations found current authorized dietary uses acceptable.
- WHO's **conditional** 2023 recommendation advises against relying on non-sugar sweeteners for long-term weight control or prevention of noncommunicable diseases. It is **not** a toxicological prohibition and expressly excludes people with pre-existing diabetes from the recommendation's scope.
- Short-duration randomized evidence indicates replacing sugar-sweetened beverages with non-sugar-sweetened beverages can modestly reduce energy intake/weight. It does **not** establish long-term protection against diabetes, cardiovascular disease, or cancer.
- Evidence for sucralose-induced microbiome/glucose changes in humans is **mixed**. Strong claims of either established harm or absence of all long-term risk overstate the evidence.
- **Preferred routine beverage:** water/unsweetened beverages. A no-sugar drink is generally a better *sugar-reduction substitute* than regular soda but need not be considered a health food.

## Regulatory thresholds (units matter)

| Sweetener | FDA ADI (mg/kg body weight/day) | EFSA ADI (mg/kg body weight/day) | Key assessment |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Sucralose (E955) | 5 | 15 | EFSA, 17 Feb 2026: current authorized uses safe; **could not confirm** proposed expanded high-temperature baking uses. |
| Acesulfame K (E950) | 15 | 15 | EFSA, 30 Apr 2025: revised prior European 9 mg/kg/day ADI to 15; estimated consumer exposures generally below ADI. |

**ADI** means an estimated amount that may be consumed daily over a lifetime without appreciable health risk. It is **not** a threshold where toxicity immediately occurs. Regional agencies can set different ADIs from different methodological judgments. Estimate total intake across all foods/drinks, not only one item.

### Example: a Pakistan Coke Zero label

One photographed local 350 mL bottle lists **sucralose 168 mg/L** and **acesulfame potassium 125 mg/L**. On the assumption those label concentrations apply to the entire bottle:

- Sucralose: 168 mg/L × 0.350 L = **58.8 mg/bottle**.
- Acesulfame K: 125 mg/L × 0.350 L = **43.75 mg/bottle**.
- At an *illustrative* 70 kg adult body weight, this is ~**16.8%** of FDA sucralose ADI (350 mg/day) and ~**4.2%** of the Ace-K ADI (1,050 mg/day).

These are **brand/country/batch-dependent label estimates**, not laboratory analyses; do not generalize to all Coke Zero formulations. ADI calculations do not account for caffeine, dental acidity, other food sources, or individual health factors.

## Human evidence by outcome

### Metabolic markers and microbiome: mixed; low-to-moderate short-term certainty

- A **2025 triple-blind, placebo-controlled trial** in healthy lean adults reported that 30 days of sucralose at 30% of the study's chosen ADI decreased an *estimated insulin-sensitivity measure* by 20.3%, with changes in microbial and inflammatory measures. Relatively small study, surrogate outcomes, limited duration; not a diabetes-incidence study. Romo-Romo et al., *Clinical Nutrition ESPEN* 2025; DOI 10.1016/j.clnesp.2025.08.029; PMID 40907790.
- A **September 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis** of ten short randomized trials (7 meta-analyzed; interventions roughly 1–10 weeks) found no statistically significant pooled effect for microbiome alpha-diversity, fasting glucose, or HOMA-IR. Sample sizes and heterogeneity constrain confidence about long-term effects. Wulandari et al., *Int J Mol Sci* 2026; DOI 10.3390/ijms27188377; PMID 42794802.
- **Interpretation:** individual biological signals warrant replication; pooled short-term null findings do not rule out effects in particular groups or over years.

### Body weight: modest short-term substitution effect, unclear durable benefit

- Meta-analysis of **19 RCTs** published January 2026: when non-nutritive sweeteners replaced caloric sugars, mean weight difference **−0.79 kg** (95% CI −1.55 to −0.04 kg), substantial heterogeneity (I² 84%); effects were clearer in studies **<18 weeks**, not significant in studies >18 weeks. Li et al., *J Endocrinol Invest* 2026; DOI 10.1007/s40618-025-02654-w; PMID 40913681.
- 2026 Korean Diabetes Association/Korean Nutrition Society umbrella/consensus review: **14 RCTs** replacing sugary drinks with artificially sweetened drinks: ~**−0.73 kg** and −0.72% body fat in studied periods; inconsistent glucose/cardiometabolic markers. Longer-term cohort associations cannot demonstrate causation. Choi et al., *Diabetes Metab J* 2026; DOI 10.4093/dmj.2025.0848; PMID 41531289.

### Cancer: no established causal link at permitted intakes; observational signals not dismissed

- NutriNet-Santé observational cohort (2022) linked higher Ace-K and aspartame intake to somewhat higher cancer incidence; residual confounding, exposure measurement error, and reverse causation remain possible. Debras et al., *PLoS Medicine* 2022; DOI 10.1371/journal.pmed.1003950; PMID 35324894.
- A 2025 review of 90 epidemiologic studies reported no consistent sweetener-specific cancer association or dose-response. **Major conflict-of-interest caution:** funded by the **American Beverage Association**, with all authors employed by consulting firms or ABA. Treat as one contested synthesis, not definitive proof of zero risk. Boon et al., *Advances in Nutrition* 2025; DOI 10.1016/j.advnut.2025.100527; PMID 41038346.
- WHO/IARC's 2023 **Group 2B** possible-carcinogen classification applied to **aspartame**, **not** the sucralose/Ace-K listed on this particular bottle. JECFA retained the aspartame ADI of 40 mg/kg/day. Hazard classification is different from exposure-specific risk.

### Heating: separate concern from cold beverages

- EFSA's **February 2026** re-evaluation affirmed approved sucralose uses but did **not** establish safety for expanded high-temperature fine-bakery applications. Prolonged high heat may create chlorinated compounds whose health significance is uncertain. This **does not establish that sucralose in a cold soft drink creates the same exposure**.
- FDA's food additive overview still describes sucralose as heat-stable; distinguish retained sweetness from potential degradation product formation under certain prolonged temperatures.

### Other considerations

- Dental erosion risk comes largely from **acidity**, including in zero-sugar cola, and is not the same as sweetener toxicity.
- **Caffeine** in cola can affect sleep and temporarily affect blood pressure; evaluate separately from artificial sweeteners.
- Formulations vary internationally. Read the exact ingredients; **aspartame, stevia, sugar alcohols, sucralose, and Ace-K are not interchangeable compounds**.

## Adversarial appraisal / research gaps

1. **Comparator matters:** substituting a high-sugar drink is not the same intervention as replacing water. Diet soda can reduce sugar vs Coke while offering no advantage vs water.
2. **Observational confounding:** people with obesity, diabetes risk, or dietary changes may select sweetened beverages because of their baseline condition. Correlation does not establish causation.
3. **RCT limitations:** short follow-up, small sample sizes, different doses/formulations, uncontrolled food sources, and surrogate endpoints limit clinical extrapolation.
4. **Sweetener specificity:** pooling all non-sugar sweeteners obscures differences in pharmacokinetics, metabolism, and exposure.
5. **Conflicts of interest:** check beverage-industry funding (and other commercial sponsorship) before giving one review dominant weight.
6. **Unknowns:** multi-year randomized effects, high-consumption subgroup outcomes, combination effects, and dose-response on clinical endpoints.
