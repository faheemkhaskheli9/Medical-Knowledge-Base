# Antimicrobial Stewardship and Common Infections

!!! warning "Local resistance matters"
    Empiric choices depend on local resistance patterns, allergies, renal function, pregnancy, and recent antibiotic use. Prefer the local/national antibiogram and guideline.

## Principles
- Many respiratory infections (colds, most sore throats, acute bronchitis, most sinusitis) are **viral** — antibiotics do not help.
- Take cultures before antibiotics when feasible in serious infections.
- Use the narrowest effective agent, shortest effective duration; review at 48–72 h.
- Antibiotics should be prescription-only; avoid leftover or shared antibiotics.

## WHO AWaRe classification
| Group | Meaning | Examples |
|-------|---------|----------|
| **Access** | First/second choice for common infections, lower resistance potential | Amoxicillin, amoxicillin-clavulanate, cefalexin, doxycycline, nitrofurantoin, metronidazole, co-trimoxazole |
| **Watch** | Higher resistance potential; use for specific indications | Ceftriaxone, azithromycin, clarithromycin, ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, piperacillin-tazobactam, meropenem, vancomycin |
| **Reserve** | Last resort for multidrug-resistant infections | Colistin, linezolid, ceftazidime-avibactam, tigecycline |

## Common infections — typical empiric options (adults)
| Infection | Usual first-line | Notes |
|-----------|------------------|-------|
| Uncomplicated cystitis (non-pregnant women) | Nitrofurantoin 5 days; fosfomycin single dose; co-trimoxazole 3 days if local resistance < 20% | Avoid nitrofurantoin in significant renal impairment; fluoroquinolones not first-line |
| Community-acquired pneumonia, outpatient, no comorbidity | Amoxicillin, or doxycycline | Macrolide only if local pneumococcal macrolide resistance < 25% |
| CAP, outpatient with comorbidity | Amoxicillin-clavulanate or cephalosporin **plus** macrolide or doxycycline; or respiratory fluoroquinolone alone | Assess severity (CURB-65) |
| Streptococcal pharyngitis (confirmed) | Penicillin V or amoxicillin, 10 days | Most sore throats are viral |
| Acute otitis media (children) | High-dose amoxicillin | Watchful waiting appropriate in selected children |
| Non-purulent cellulitis | Beta-lactam active against streptococci (cefalexin, flucloxacillin) | Mark the margin; elevate the limb |
| Purulent skin infection / abscess | Incision and drainage; consider MRSA-active agent | |
| Acute bacterial sinusitis | Only if ≥ 10 days, severe, or worsening after improvement; amoxicillin ± clavulanate | |

## Resistance alerts
- **XDR typhoid** (Pakistan, from 2016): resistant to chloramphenicol, ampicillin, co-trimoxazole, fluoroquinolones, and third-generation cephalosporins → azithromycin (uncomplicated) or carbapenem (complicated). See [Pakistan](../regional/pakistan.md).
- **Fluoroquinolones**: boxed warnings (tendinopathy, neuropathy, CNS effects, aortic aneurysm) — reserve when alternatives exist.
- **Clostridioides difficile** risk is highest with clindamycin, fluoroquinolones, cephalosporins, and broad-spectrum agents.
