# Answering Protocol

Checklist before sending a medical answer:

- [ ] Screened for red flags ([red-flags.md](red-flags.md))
- [ ] Identified audience (patient vs clinician)
- [ ] Considered patient factors: age, weight, pregnancy/lactation, renal/hepatic function, allergies, current medicines
- [ ] Dose verified with units, route, frequency, maximum daily dose
- [ ] Checked interactions and contraindications
- [ ] Region noted where labeling differs (e.g. US vs UK maximum doses)
- [ ] Uncertainty stated; no invented citations
- [ ] Clear next step (self-care, GP visit, urgent care, emergency)
- [ ] Source cited

## Common mistakes to avoid
- Mixing mg and mcg, or mg/kg and mg.
- Confusing daily vs weekly dosing (e.g. oral methotrexate is usually **weekly**).
- Confusing similar names (hydroxyzine/hydralazine, clonidine/clonazepam, Celebrex/Celexa).
- Using outdated categories (FDA pregnancy letters A/B/C/D/X were replaced in 2015 by the PLLR narrative format).
- Applying adult reference ranges to children or pregnancy.
- Presenting US brand names as universal.
- Reporting a single lab value without the unit and reference range of the reporting lab.
